# Using and maintaining the checking aid

The design, the implementation plan and the evaluation protocol are in the thesis repository (Luuuuuig/Bachelor-End-Project, folder `docs/`).
This manual was written for the state of the code at commit 75207cc of the thesis repository's work branch and updated for Task 25 (the pre-check in the browser) on 8 November 2026; where the code changes, the code is right and this manual must follow. The aid README (`aid/README.md`) has the install and the "Try it out" steps on the made-up set.

## 1. What the aid does

The checking aid is a Python program that checks orders for the buyer. It does two checks.

- **The post-check.** A supplier sends an order confirmation. The aid compares it with the purchase order (PO): every line, the price, the quantity, the unit, the delivery date, missing or extra lines and the required information (the Incoterm).
- **The pre-check.** Before ordering in a webshop, a browser extension sends the cart page the buyer has open to the aid's capture server on the same computer. The aid compares the cart with the PO at once (price, quantity, unit and whether each line is in the cart) and the extension shows the result in a banner above the page, before the buyer presses order. A cart page saved from the browser can still go through the batch command instead (section 2.5).

**The flow of a confirmation.**

1. The e-mails for the aid arrive in a folder, the inbox. After a sign-in with the buyer's own Microsoft account on the settings tab of the review page, the runner reads one folder of the work mailbox itself and saves each new e-mail there (section 3.14); without a sign-in, a mailbox rule saves them there, as before (section 3.3).
2. The runner looks at the inbox every 60 seconds. It files PO PDFs as versions of their PO. For each order confirmation it opens a case. Other documents (packing slips, invoices, quotations) are skipped and listed in the digest.
3. The aid reads the confirmation, picks the PO version it answers, pairs its lines with the PO lines and compares the values by fixed rules. It reads with the AI service, Microsoft 365 Copilot, used with the buyer's own work sign-in (section 3.15), or without AI with the template parser (section 3.12). Copilot reads the text layer of a PDF only: a document without one (a scan) is not read, and its case goes to the buyer, listed as failed in the digest.
4. The routing decides per finding: the aid makes the change itself (AUTO), asks the supplier a standard question (ASK), or hands the item to the buyer (REVIEW).
5. The aid makes the routine changes, sends the questions and records everything in the case folder.
6. Once a day the buyer gets a digest: what the aid did, with an undo link next to each change it made in the system, and a link to the review page for each case that waits for him.

**The rules as the buyer experiences them.** The rule codes appear only in the records, never on the page or in the digest. The settings each rule reads are named in brackets.

| Rule | What happens |
|---|---|
| R11 | A new supplier (fewer closed cases than `first_cases_review`) or a supplier the buyer marked (`review_suppliers`): the whole case comes to the buyer, whatever the findings. |
| R09 | The aid could not pick a PO version, or could not compare a value (another currency, a total only, an uncertain match, an unreadable value and similar): the item comes to the buyer. |
| R02 | The delivery date is earlier than the PO date, or later but within the supplier's lead time plus the tolerance (`supplier_lead_times`, `lead_time_tolerance_days`): the aid writes the new date on the PO line. |
| R03 | The delivery date is later than that, missing on the document, the lead time is unknown, or the line is split: the item comes to the buyer. |
| R04 | The price differs by at most `price_pct` percent of the PO price and at most `price_abs` euro on the line: the aid writes the new price on the PO line and on the article. |
| R05 | The price differs by more, is missing, the quantity is unknown, or the line is split: the item comes to the buyer. |
| R06 | The quantity or the unit differs: the aid asks the supplier (`messages_off_suppliers`). |
| R07 | A line is missing or extra: the aid asks the supplier. A line that is not in a cart comes to the buyer. |
| R08 | Required information is missing or different: the aid asks the supplier. |
| R10 | The PO is above the authorization limit (`authorization_limit`): the aid makes its date or price change and asks the approver to approve the PO again, with the buyer in copy. When a limit is set and the PO total is unknown, the change is not made and the item comes to the buyer. |
| R01 | When nothing waits for the buyer or the supplier, the aid prepares the confirmation to attach to the PO and its status from Besteld to Bevestigd. When a question or a review item is open, it prepares the confirmation to attach only, if it prepared other changes. Neither is a row to tick on the tab "In Exact invoeren": the tab lists the confirmations to attach at its bottom for 7 days after the aid last prepared the attachment or the status (section 2.6), the digest marks both "(zelf in Exact doen)", and the buyer attaches the confirmation and sets the status Bevestigd in Exact himself, as he does today (section 2.1). |
| R00 | A finding that no rule covers comes to the buyer. |

On a cart, every question of R06, R07 and R08 comes to the buyer instead ("cart: for the buyer"), and nothing is attached and no status is set. R04 works on a cart as on a confirmation: a small price difference is written on the PO line's price and on the article price (or prepared, while no route to Exact exists), before the order is placed, and the banner says so. For a supplier in `messages_off_suppliers` the questions come to the buyer instead ("messages off for this supplier").

**What the aid never does.**

- It never creates, releases or sends a PO, and never pays anything.
- It changes nothing but the PO line's price, delivery date and quantity, the article's purchase price, the attached confirmation and the confirmation status.
- It sends a supplier only the three standard questions, always with the buyer in copy, and the approver only the request to approve a PO again.
- It only reads the folder of the work mailbox it is connected to: it never moves, flags or deletes a message there. When it sends through that mailbox, each message is first a draft there, which then goes to Sent Items (section 3.14).
- It never says "no difference" when it could not compare a value.
- It never makes a change without a line in the write log that holds the old value, so that the change can be undone.
- In the Bachelor End Project it never writes to the live system: only to the copy of the test account, or as prepared changes.
- It never sends a company document, or anything taken from one, to an AI service outside the company's Microsoft tenant.

## 2. For the buyer

### 2.1 The daily digest

The digest is an e-mail to the buyer, from the aid's mailbox, or from the work mailbox when the aid sends through it (section 3.14). Its subject is "[Inkoopassistent] Dagoverzicht DD-MM-YYYY" (in English "[Inkoopassistent] Daily digest DD-MM-YYYY"). It comes once a day, at the first pass of the runner at or after `digest_time` (16:30 by default), or, when `digest` is `per_case`, after every pass that has a case or a skipped file to report. If the company allows a Power Automate flow, the same text also arrives in Teams (section 3.9).

The digest has up to five blocks. An empty block is left out.

| Block (Dutch) | Block (English) | What it lists |
|---|---|---|
| Behandelde zaken | Handled cases | Every case handled since the last digest, one line each: PO, supplier, status and the number of changes. Under it, a line per change: the field, the line or article, old value → new value. A change made in the system has a link "ongedaan maken" (undo). A prepared change of a price, a delivery date, a quantity or an article's purchase price says "(voorbereid)": it is not in Exact yet and stands on the tab "In Exact invoeren" (section 2.6). The confirmation to attach and the status Bevestigd say "(zelf in Exact doen)" (do it in Exact yourself): the aid prepares them and shows them, without a tick, at the bottom of the tab "In Exact invoeren" for 7 days after the aid last prepared the attachment or the status (section 2.6), and the case's line counts them apart, as "in Exact zelf te doen" ("PO 250101 (S01): afgesloten, 2 in Exact zelf te doen"). A failed one says "(mislukt)". None of these changed anything, so there is nothing to undo. |
| Wacht op u | Waiting for you | The cases in review, each with a link "beoordelen" (review) and a line per item with its reason, field and line. |
| Vraag gesteld aan de leverancier | Question sent to the supplier | The cases that wait for a supplier's answer, each with the PO, the supplier and the kind of each question (for example "aantal of eenheid"). |
| Mislukt | Failed | The cases the aid could not finish, with the first line of the error. Tell the researcher. |
| Overgeslagen | Skipped | Files in the inbox that opened no case, with the reason (for example "invoice", "duplicate", "more than one PO number"). |

A day without anything says "Er zijn geen zaken behandeld en geen bestanden overgeslagen." Under the title, before the blocks, the first line says "In Exact in te voeren: 2 wijzigingen. Open het tabblad In Exact invoeren: <link>" while changes wait on the review page's tab "In Exact invoeren" (section 2.6); its link opens the page on that tab. When the list cannot be read (a file of it is held by another program, or damaged), that line says instead "De lijst In Exact invoeren kon niet worden gelezen. Open het tabblad om te zien wat er nog in te voeren is: <link>", so that a day with changes waiting never looks like a day with nothing to enter. A line says "Postvak niet verbonden" while the mailbox's sign-in has lapsed (section 5), and a line "Copilot werkt niet (<the error>): <n> zaken wachten erop" while Copilot fails, with the number of cases waiting for it (section 3.15). A pre-check case begins with the word "voorcontrole". Prices use the decimal comma in Dutch.

**The links.** "beoordelen" opens the review page with that case selected. "ongedaan maken" opens the page with that case and that change selected; nothing is undone until you press the undo button on the page. The links work only in a browser on the computer where the review page runs (section 3.8).

**You enter the changes in Exact.** The aid changes nothing in Exact itself (section 3.10): every change of a price, a delivery date, a quantity or an article's purchase price that the aid makes, and every one you confirm on the review page, is a prepared change, marked "(voorbereid)", and stands on the tab "In Exact invoeren" until you have entered it in Exact and ticked it, the aid saw it in a new PDF of the purchase order or in an export from Exact, or you chose not to enter it (section 2.6). The confirmation to attach and the status Bevestigd are no rows to tick: where the digest says "(zelf in Exact doen)", attach the confirmation and set the status Bevestigd in Exact yourself, as you do today; the tab "In Exact invoeren" shows each confirmation at its bottom for 7 days after the aid last prepared the attachment or the status, so that a status prepared at a later close shows there as well. The digest then has no undo links: a prepared change is not in Exact, so there is nothing to undo.

**A try-out in the digest.** A case made from a try-out confirmation (section 3.17) has " (proefdocument)" behind its line, in every block, and each of its rows says "(proefdocument)" in place of "(voorbereid)" and "(zelf in Exact doen)": "PO 250108 (S08): afgesloten, 4 voorbereide wijzigingen (proefdocument)". Do nothing in the real Exact for it: it is made up. The first line's count "In Exact in te voeren" leaves its changes out. So it is in the test mode and after the test mode was switched off, and in the English digest "(try-out document)".

### 2.2 The review page

**Opening it.** Click a link in the digest, or open http://127.0.0.1:7860 in a browser on the computer where the page runs. If that port is taken, the command takes the next free one and prints the address, and `review_url` must then name that port. The page is in Dutch. The switch "Taal" at the top changes it to English ("Nederlands" / "English"). The switch changes the page only; the digest and the questions follow the language of the settings. The page has three tabs: "Beoordelen" (this section), "In Exact invoeren" (section 2.6) and "Instellingen" (section 2.7). The link of the digest's line "In Exact in te voeren" opens the page on the tab "In Exact invoeren".

**The case list.** The dropdown "Zaak" (Case) shows the cases in review, and any case with a change made in the system that is not undone yet. Each case reads like "PO 250106 (S06), 2026-11-05 14:18, 3 punten": the PO, the supplier, the time the case was opened and the number of items to decide. A case that is not in review also shows its status. A case made from a try-out confirmation (section 3.17) has "(proefdocument)" behind it, so that it never looks like a supplier's confirmation of the same order. The button "Lijst vernieuwen" (Refresh the list) reloads the list. Cases that wait for a supplier, and closed or failed cases without an undoable change, are not in the list.

**The two documents.** Side by side: "Document van de leverancier" (the supplier's document, page by page) and "Inkooporder" (the PO version the aid chose). A cart saved as a web page, and a PO taken from an ERP export, have no pages to show; their values are in the lists.

**The three lists.**

- "Afwijkingen" (Differences): the differences, and the items the buyer must decide.
- "Overeenkomsten" (Matches): the values that agree.
- "Niet te vergelijken" (Could not compare): the values the aid could not compare, with the reason.

Each list has the columns Punt (item), Veld (field), Regel (line), Inkooporder (the PO value), Leverancier (the supplier's value), Reden (reason), Bron inkooporder and Bron leverancier (where the value stands: page, line and the text of that line). Items to decide are numbered RI1, RI2 and so on.

**The four decisions.** Under "Besluiten" (Decisions) each item has a row with the choice "Besluit". A choice is saved at once ("Besluit over RI1 opgeslagen."); nothing is prepared or written until you close the case. A close prepares its changes, of which a price, a delivery date, a quantity or an article's purchase price then stands on the tab "In Exact invoeren" for you to enter in Exact (section 2.6), and files its e-mails in cases/outbox/; nothing goes to Exact and no mail is sent from the page (section 3.7). The last choice on an item counts.

| Decision | What the close does |
|---|---|
| Bevestigen: waarde van de leverancier overnemen (Confirm: take the supplier's value) | On a price, delivery date or quantity of one PO line: prepares the supplier's value for the PO line, a row on the tab "In Exact invoeren". On any other item it writes nothing: handle it outside the aid. On an item about the whole case (for example "new supplier"; it names no finding) it means "seen, fine". |
| Waarde van de inkooporder houden (Keep the purchase order's value) | Writes nothing and sends nothing. On an item about the whole case it also means "seen, fine". |
| Leverancier vragen (Ask the supplier) | Prepares the standard question for that field, buyer in copy, one e-mail per kind of question. Today the page does not send it: it writes the e-mail as an .eml file in cases/outbox/ (section 3.7) and the researcher sends it by hand. The case then waits for the answer. Not offered on an item about the whole case (it has no field, for example "new supplier"), nor on a cart. |
| Onopgelost (Unresolved) | Nothing happens; the case stays in review. |

**The article tick.** On a price item the tick "Ook de artikelprijs bijwerken" (Also update the article price) appears. With the tick and "Bevestigen", the close also prepares the new price for the article, a row "artikel <code>: inkoopprijs ..." after the rows of the orders on the tab "In Exact invoeren". The tick is set already when the aid had tried that article change itself and it waits for you.

**The close.** The button "Zaak afsluiten" (Close the case) carries out the decisions. The page then says the new status, for example "De zaak is nu: afgesloten." The case closes when every item is "Bevestigen" or "Houden", nothing went wrong and no question to the supplier is open. Then the aid prepares the confirmation to attach and the status Bevestigd, which the tab "In Exact invoeren" shows at its bottom, without a tick, for 7 days after the aid last prepared the attachment or the status: attach the confirmation and set the status Bevestigd in Exact yourself (the digest says "(zelf in Exact doen)"). The case waits for the supplier when an item is on "Leverancier vragen". It stays in review when an item is undecided or "Onopgelost", or a message could not be sent.

What a close writes, for the record: each change in the write log of the case and in the prepared changes (the page always uses the prepared writer, section 3.7); the closes and undos in `steps/09_close.json` and in the audit record; a row per decision in the case's `dispositions.csv`. Your choices themselves are in `decisions.jsonl`. A second close never repeats a change or a question that was made already.

**Messages after a close.**

- "Een schrijfactie is niet gelukt (...): ... De zaak blijft ter beoordeling." (A write did not go through.) The close stopped at that change; the reason is "De schrijfactie is geweigerd" (The write was refused) or the label of the item it became, such as "Voorbereide wijziging: met de hand doorvoeren".
- "Een bericht is niet verzonden; de zaak blijft ter beoordeling." (A message was not sent.) Usually the supplier has no e-mail address in the settings. Tell the researcher.
- "Vraag aan de leverancier staat open." (A question to the supplier is open.) The PO is not confirmed while the supplier may still answer.
- "Deze zaak kan niet worden afgesloten: zij is niet meer ter beoordeling." The case was closed or changed in the meantime. Refresh the list.
- "Dit besluit kan hier niet worden genomen." (This decision cannot be taken here.) For example "Leverancier vragen" on an item about the whole case or on a cart (the page does not offer it there).

**The undo.** Below the close button: choose the change under "Schrijfactie" (Write) and press "Ongedaan maken" (Undo). Only changes a program made in Exact are offered; a prepared change is not in Exact, so it is not offered here, and while every change is prepared the list "Schrijfactie" stays empty. A prepared change you do not want, you drop on the tab "In Exact invoeren" ("Aangevinkte regels niet invoeren", section 2.6). The page says "Ongedaan gemaakt: ..." (Undone) when the undo was made in Exact, or "Niets ongedaan gemaakt: ..." when the change was undone already, or "Ongedaan maken is mislukt." when the undo failed. "Kies eerst een schrijfactie." means no change was chosen. Note for the operator: the review page, as its command builds it, uses the prepared writer, so an undo from the page is a prepared change too, which then stands on the list with "Terugzetten:" before it, and the page says "Nog niet teruggedraaid in Exact. Op de lijst staat nu: ..." instead of "Ongedaan gemaakt" (sections 2.6 and 3.10). A change that is in Exact and must go back, you put back from the tab "In Exact invoeren" with "Terugdraaien in Exact" (section 2.6).

**The re-approval banner.** "Deze wijziging stuurt de inkooporder opnieuw door de fiattering" (This change sends the purchase order through approval again) shows on a PO above the authorization limit. A close that changes a PO line then also asks the approver to approve the PO again, with you in copy.

**A case that waits for its purchase order.** Above the documents of a confirmation that names a purchase order the aid does not know yet, the page says, with the number the confirmation names: "Inkooporder 250101 kent de assistent nog niet. Komt die inkooporder uit Exact binnen, dan controleert de assistent deze bevestiging vanzelf opnieuw; u hoeft hier niets te besluiten. Blijft zij weg, sleep dan de pdf van de inkooporder of de e-mail van Exact naar de map die de assistent leest. Is 250101 niet een van uw inkoopordernummers, besluit dan zelf over deze zaak." When the aid knows the order but not the version the confirmation quotes, it says: "Versie 1 van inkooporder 250103 kent de assistent nog niet. Komt die versie uit Exact binnen, dan controleert de assistent deze bevestiging vanzelf opnieuw; u hoeft hier niets te besluiten. Blijft zij weg, sleep dan de pdf van die versie of de e-mail van Exact naar de map die de assistent leest." The aid checks such a case again when that order, or that version, is filed: it checks the confirmation as a new case, and this case leaves the list (section 3.17). At the first pass after the runner starts every order of the store counts as filed, so a case is checked again then only when its order, or the version it quotes, is in the store. A decision you record on the case ends this: the hint goes at once, the aid no longer checks the case again by itself, and you decide on it as on any case. "Lijst vernieuwen" also hides the hint of a case decided in another browser tab. A confirmation on which the aid found no order number has no hint and waits for nothing: decide on it yourself (section 3.17 says when the aid still links it by itself). A confirmation whose order the aid found otherwise than by the number recognition (the reason of a guessed order, section 2.4) has no hint either and is not counted, as a guess makes no promise. When the aid does not know the version such a confirmation quotes, its case also has "Geen versie van de inkooporder gekozen": nothing was compared, and the order pane ("Inkooporder") stays empty because no version was chosen. Until you decide on such a case, the aid checks it again once that version is filed.

**Other messages.** "Kies eerst een zaak." means no case is chosen. "De besluiten van deze zaak kunnen niet worden gelezen (decisions.jsonl)." means the file with your choices is damaged; tell the researcher.

### 2.3 The supplier questions and the wait

The aid asks only three standard questions: about a quantity or unit ("aantal of eenheid"), about a missing or extra line ("ontbrekende of extra regel"), and about missing information ("ontbrekende gegevens"). Each is an e-mail to the supplier, from the aid's mailbox or from the work mailbox (section 3.14), the buyer in copy, with the subject "Vraag over orderbevestiging PO <number>" and a table of the lines (line, item code, description, our value, your value). It asks for an answer by reply within the wait, three working days by default; you set it on the settings tab ("Wachttijd op een antwoord", section 2.7).

- **The supplier replies** to the e-mail: the aid files the reply into the case, and the case comes back to you with the item "supplier answered: read the reply". The page shows this reason as "Andere reden" (Another reason), and the digest shows the English text. The page does not show the reply itself: read it in your mailbox (you were in copy, so a reply to all reaches you). If the answer needs a change, make it by hand, then choose "Bevestigen" or "Houden" on the item and close the case.
- **The e-mail bounces**: the case comes back at once with "Bericht kwam terug" (Message bounced).
- **No answer in time**: after the wait (Saturday and Sunday are skipped; public holidays are not) the case comes back with "Geen antwoord binnen de wachttijd" (No answer within the wait).

An out-of-office reply changes nothing. After a question the aid asked itself, the differences stay in the list "Afwijkingen" without a decision row. Handle them outside the aid if needed, then decide on the new item (without a finding) and close. After a question you asked from the page, the item you decided on keeps its row, still on "Leverancier vragen": change it to "Bevestigen" or "Houden" before you close, or the case stays in review. A case that comes back is listed again under "Wacht op u" in the next digest.

### 2.5 The pre-check in the browser

**The extension.** A small extension for Edge or Chrome (`aid/extension`, loaded as its README describes) watches the pages of the webshops listed in its options. When a page holds a cart table (a table with an item column, a quantity column and a price column), it sends a copy of the page to the aid's capture server on the same computer, once per change of the cart: when the page opens, and again about a second after a quantity or a line changes. The server opens a cart case, checks it like any other case and answers at once. The case lands in the case folders like any other: on the review page when it waits for the buyer, and in the next digest under the pre-check ("voorcontrole"), which the runner makes when it runs on the same folder (section 3.13).

**The banner.** The answer appears in one banner at the top of the page. Its title is fixed, in Dutch with the English in brackets; the lines under it come from the aid in the language of the settings.

- **Green**, "Winkelwagen komt overeen met de inkooporder": the cart agrees with its PO, or the only differences were small price differences that the aid settled itself (R04). Those lines are listed under the title. While no route to Exact exists every change is prepared, and the line reads "regel 2 (KM-2102): prijs op de inkooporder: wijziging voorbereid van 4.10 naar 4.15, zie de zaak (prijs binnen de drempel)"; once changes are written to Exact it reads "regel 2 (KM-2102): prijs op de inkooporder bijgewerkt van 4.10 naar 4.15 (prijs binnen de drempel)". A case the buyer closed on the review page is green only when every difference is settled as it stands now: a line the buyer decided counts by the buyer's latest decision (taking the cart's value, with its change prepared or made and not undone, whoever made it; a change left to apply by hand does not count; keeping the PO's value leaves the difference), and a line the buyer did not decide by the change the aid's rules wrote or prepared; a line whose change was undone counts as a difference again ("wijziging ongedaan gemaakt"): undone in Exact by a program, or put back from the tab "In Exact invoeren" and that put-back entered or seen there; while the put-back still waits on that list, Exact holds the change, the line counts it as made and says "terugzetten staat op de lijst In Exact invoeren". The list decides this for every case that asked the change: when the same cart was checked again on another day, its later case is told of a put-back made through the first case's change as well. While the list cannot be read (another program holds `prepared_changes.csv` or `marks.jsonl`), no prepared change counts as made, whatever case answers and whether it was put back or not: the line shows the difference between the cart and the order, with "de lijst In Exact invoeren kon niet worden gelezen", and the banner is amber until the list can be read again.
- **Amber**, "Let op: de winkelwagen wijkt af van de inkooporder": one line per difference, in the order of the lines, for example "regel 2 (KM-2002): prijs 41.00 in de winkelwagen, 40.00 op de inkooporder (prijsverschil boven de drempel)", "regel 3 (KM-2003): kon niet in de winkelwagen worden geplaatst" or "regel 4 (KM-2004): extra regel, niet op de inkooporder". A reason about the whole case is a line of its own, for example "eerste zaken van deze leverancier gaan naar de inkoper". A small price difference that the aid wrote or prepared (R04) shows as such, with the text of the green banner, and is not counted as a difference: do not correct it again. A reason the aid has no wording for reads "zie de zaak voor de reden". When the buyer decided on a line on the review page, the line says so ("besluit van de inkoper: waarde van de inkooporder gehouden"). Correct the cart, or the PO, before ordering.
- **Grey**, "Geen inkooporder gevonden": the cart names no PO the aid knows, and its item codes match none.
- **Red**, "Voorcontrole mislukt" when the check failed (the case's run failed, or the server met an error, with "the pre-check failed; see capture.log"), "Inkoopassistent niet bereikbaar" when the capture server does not run, "Inkoopassistent weigert de pagina" with the server's reason when it refused the page (a wrong token, or "no cart table in ..." for a page the cart reader cannot read). When the extension was updated while the page was open, the title is "Voorcontrole mislukt" with the line "Herlaad de pagina (reload the page)" under it: no check ran and no case exists; reloading the page is all it needs.

Under the title a sentence sums the case up ("Inkooporder 250202: 1 regel komt overeen, 1 verschil en 1 regel niet in de winkelwagen"); the lines follow, and under them the link "Bekijk de zaak". The link selects the case on the review page only while the case waits for the buyer (in review) or holds a change made in Exact that can be undone; any other case, a green or a failed one, or an amber one the buyer already closed, is read in its case folder.

**A reload opens no new case.** The same page sent again, byte for byte, is answered from its case on any day; the same cart in a page whose invisible parts changed (a time stamp, an advertisement) is answered from the case it opened that day. No second case is opened. A changed cart (another quantity, a line more or less) is a new case. A case that failed is never reused.

**The fallback.** A cart page saved from the browser as a web page (HTML) can still go through the batch command with its PO number (section 6), as before the extension existed. The watched folder does not take cart pages. A cart saved as a screenshot (PNG) cannot be read: Copilot, the AI service, reads the text layer of a document only (section 3.15), so save the cart page as HTML. On a cart, every difference comes to the buyer; "Leverancier vragen" is not offered, because the buyer corrects the cart or the PO before ordering.

### 2.6 The tab In Exact invoeren

The aid changes nothing in Exact itself: from your seat no program reaches Exact. Every change the aid makes by its rules, and every change you confirm on the tab "Beoordelen", is prepared, and the tab "In Exact invoeren" (Enter in Exact) of the review page lists what you enter in Exact. Its label counts the changes still to enter, "In Exact invoeren (3)"; without a number nothing waits, and "In Exact invoeren (?)" means that the list could not be read (below). The digest's first line gives the same number with the link to this tab (section 2.1).

**The list.** Under the line "De assistent verandert zelf niets in Exact. Hieronder staat wat u in Exact invoert. Vink aan wat u hebt ingevoerd." each change is one row with a tick box, one under the other. The rows come per purchase order, in the order of the order numbers, by line and then price, delivery date and quantity, and after them the article prices by article code. A row names its order, the line, the article of that line in the version of the order its case was compared with, and the old and the new value: "Inkooporder 250108 (S08), regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,50 → 12,70", "Inkooporder 250114 (S14), regel 1 (KM-1401): leverdatum 16-11-2026 → week 48 (2026)" or "artikel KM-0801: inkoopprijs onbekend → 12,70". A price or a quantity has the decimal comma, a day reads as dd-mm-yyyy and a week as "week 48 (2026)"; "onbekend" is a value the aid does not know, such as the old purchase price of an article it never read. Only the price, the delivery date and the quantity of an order line and the purchase price of an article are rows: attaching the confirmation and setting the status to Bevestigd are not on the list. The aid prepares them too, and the digest marks them "(zelf in Exact doen)": attach the confirmation and set the status Bevestigd in Exact yourself, as you do today. The tab shows them at its bottom, without a tick, under "Bevestigd door de leverancier" (below). With nothing to enter the tab says "Er is niets in te voeren." While the aid is in the test mode (section 3.17), the line under the intro says "Proefstand: voer deze wijzigingen alleen in het test-Exact in.": the rows then come from the test account of Exact, and from try-out confirmations, and go into the test Exact only. That line shows whenever the test mode is on, whatever the authorization limit, until the test mode is switched off on the settings tab. A change that only try-out confirmations asked carries "(proefdocument)" behind its row, in the test mode and out of it: "Inkooporder 250108 (S08), regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,50 → 12,63 (proefdocument)". It is made up: never enter it in the real Exact; once the test is over, tick it and press "Aangevinkte regels niet invoeren".

**One row per change.** The same change asked by several cases (two confirmations of one order, three captures of one cart) is one row, and an article price is one row whichever order or price list asked it. When a newer document asks another value for the same line, an older change still open leaves the list and the newer one is the row. A change you chose not to enter is not asked again for the same value, as long as no other value was asked for that line in between, and a change is not asked while your ticks say that Exact holds its value already. When the changes of a case are undone (section 3.10), a change of it that you had not entered goes off the list, since Exact still holds the value from before; for one you had entered, a row asks you to put the old value back. A change that another case asked too stays on the list for that case.

**Ticking.** Enter the change in Exact first. Then tick its row and press "Aangevinkte regels zijn ingevoerd": the page says "1 wijziging genoteerd als ingevoerd in Exact." (or "2 wijzigingen genoteerd als ingevoerd in Exact.") and the row leaves the list. A change you do not want in Exact, tick and press "Aangevinkte regels niet invoeren": "1 wijziging vervalt: de assistent vraagt er niet meer om." The two buttons stand above the rows and again below them, so that they stay in reach of a long list. To tick a whole order at once, choose it under "Blok" (the article prices are the choice "Artikelprijzen") and press "Alles van dit blok is ingevoerd": it notes the rows of that order as the tab showed them when the list was last read, and a change that came in after that stays on the list. With nothing ticked or chosen the page says "Vink eerst aan wat u bedoelt." and notes nothing. When none of the rows you pressed for is still open (another browser tab handled them, or a newer document replaced the value), the page says "Er is niets genoteerd: de aangevinkte regels staan niet meer open (al afgehandeld, of vervangen door een nieuwere waarde). De lijst is bijgewerkt." and notes nothing either. The list is read again when you select the tab, after each button and with "Lijst vernieuwen". A tick or a drop given by mistake is taken back under the list, with "Weer op de lijst zetten" (below).

**Seen by the aid itself.** After you enter a change, the order must be approved and released again in Exact, and Exact then e-mails a new PDF of the purchase order. The runner files it as a new version of the order, and at each pass, after the cases ran, it compares every open row of an order line with the order as it stands: its newest PDF, or an export from Exact given after it. A row whose new value that look shows leaves the list by itself, marked "(gezien in versie 2 van de inkooporder)", or "(gezien in een export uit Exact)" for an export, and you tick nothing: a price when the line's net price (after its discount, per its own price unit), rounded to the decimals of the new value and at least two, equals it; a delivery date when the new PDF's date lies within the new value, so that a day inside the new week counts; a quantity when the two are equal as numbers. The line is found by its number when its article agrees with the article of that line in the version the case compared with, else by its article when exactly one line holds it. A PDF or an export counts only when the aid filed it after the change was asked, and for a row that puts a change back after that row. What counts is when the aid filed it, not when Exact made it: a print that reaches the aid late, because the aid was stopped, the sign-in of the mailbox had lapsed, or a PDF was saved and given to the aid later, counts as new and can tick a change or a put-back off, also when Exact printed it before you entered the change. So when the aid was stopped for a while, or the sign-in of the mailbox had lapsed, check in Exact, once it runs again or the sign-in works again, the put-backs it ticked off as seen. An article price is never ticked off by a purchase order. What a later PDF shows never takes back a tick, a drop or a change the aid saw. When such a look shows neither the old nor the new value, the row stays on the list with a note after it: "Inkooporder 250108 (S08), regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,50 → 12,70. Exact toont nu 12,80." Whether the test Exact sends such a PDF after a change is the owner's check A7; when it does not, you tick the row as before.

**Handled changes.** Under the list, the section "Afgehandeld in de laatste 14 dagen" (Handled in the last 14 days) names every change entered, seen or dropped in that time, the newest first, with how the aid knows it: "(door u afgevinkt op 18-11-2026)", "(gezien in versie 2 van de inkooporder)", "(gezien in een export uit Exact)" or "(niet ingevoerd, op 18-11-2026)". Choose one under "Afgehandelde wijziging" and press one of the two buttons beside it.

- "Weer op de lijst zetten" takes back your own tick or drop: "De regel staat weer op de lijst." and the aid asks for the change again. A change the aid saw itself is not taken back: "Deze wijziging heeft de assistent zelf in Exact gezien. Kies Terugdraaien in Exact als de oude waarde terug moet." A change you put back with "Terugdraaien in Exact" does not return either, since you asked for the old value: "Deze wijziging is teruggedraaid; zij komt niet terug op de lijst." The page says "De wijziging komt niet terug op de lijst: er is intussen een andere wijziging voor dezelfde regel of hetzelfde artikel." only when another change for that line or article came in after it. Nothing is written then. With no change chosen under "Afgehandelde wijziging", either button says "Kies eerst een wijziging bij Afgehandelde wijziging." and writes nothing.
- "Terugdraaien in Exact" is for a change that is in Exact (ticked or seen) and must go back. The aid prepares the old value as a new row, which stands on the list with "Terugzetten:" before it, "Inkooporder 250108 (S08), Terugzetten: regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,70 → 12,50", and says "Nog niet teruggedraaid in Exact. Op de lijst staat nu: Inkooporder 250108 (S08), Terugzetten: regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,70 → 12,50. Voer de oude waarde in Exact in en vink de regel af." The page never says "Ongedaan gemaakt" for it. The change stays among the handled ones as it was, next to its put-back, also after you entered the put-back and ticked it (or the aid saw it in a new PDF or an export). A second press says "Het terugzetten staat al op de lijst."; when you had dropped the put-back, it is put on the list again; when the put-back is entered or seen, a press on the change says "Deze wijziging is al teruggedraaid." A put-back is a row of its own on the list: it never joins another row of the same value. When a newer change for the same line came in after the put-back, it replaced the put-back on the list, also one you had dropped, as a newer value replaces any older change; while that newer change is on the list, or is entered or seen and Exact still holds it, a press says "De wijziging komt niet terug op de lijst: er is intussen een andere wijziging voor dezelfde regel of hetzelfde artikel.", and once it no longer stands in the way (you dropped it, it only asked a value Exact holds already, or it was put back itself), a press makes a new put-back. A put-back never replaces a newer change: while a newer change for the same line or article, which another confirmation asked, is open, entered or seen, the page refuses, writes nothing and says how to go on, unless the marks say that Exact holds this change's old value already: then it says "Deze wijziging is al teruggedraaid." While the newer change is on the list: "Terugdraaien kan nu niet: er staat een nieuwere wijziging op de lijst, Inkooporder 250108 (S08), regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,70 → 12,90. Laat die eerst vallen; daarna kunt u deze wijziging terugdraaien." Once it is entered or seen: "Terugdraaien kan nu niet: er is een nieuwere wijziging ingevoerd, Inkooporder 250108 (S08), regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,70 → 12,90. Draai die eerst terug en vink het terugzetten af; daarna kunt u deze wijziging terugdraaien." When the newer change asks this change's old value itself, nothing needs to be put back: while it is on the list, "Terugdraaien is niet nodig: op de lijst staat al een wijziging naar de oude waarde, Inkooporder 250108 (S08), regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,70 → 12,50. Voer die in Exact in en vink die af."; once it is entered or seen, "Deze wijziging is al teruggedraaid." A newer change that the aid can never put back says so, and what to do: "Terugdraaien kan nu niet: er is een nieuwere wijziging ingevoerd, artikel KM-0801: inkoopprijs onbekend → 12,90, die de assistent niet kan terugdraaien. Zet de oude waarde zelf terug in Exact." The newer change stops blocking once you dropped it, or once its own put-back is entered or seen, or once the marks say that Exact no longer holds its value (a row entered after it set another value); a press then makes the put-back of this change, which stands on the list, or says "Deze wijziging is al teruggedraaid." when the marks say that Exact holds its old value already (another put-back or a newer change entered it). Before the page makes a put-back it works out that the put-back will stand on the list; should it ever say "Terugdraaien kan nu niet: het terugzetten zou niet op de lijst komen. Er is niets geschreven; geef dit door aan de onderzoeker.", nothing was written: pass it on to the researcher. A change that can never be put back says so first, before any newer change. An article price that a confirmation asked has no price before it that the aid knows: "Van dit artikel kent de assistent de oude prijs niet; zet die zelf terug in Exact." An article price of a price list has one, and is put back like an order line. A change you did not enter has nothing to put back ("Deze wijziging is niet ingevoerd; in Exact is er niets terug te draaien."), and a put-back is not put back in turn ("De assistent kan deze wijziging niet terugdraaien; zet de oude waarde zelf terug in Exact.").

A put-back is the undo of that one change: it is recorded in the case's write log, its `steps/09_close.json` and its audit record, or for a price list in the list's own write log (section 3.10), and one line in `marks.jsonl` links the new row to the change it puts back. A put-back made again, once newer rows came after the first one and no longer ask anything, is a second undo of the same change, recorded the same way.

**Bevestigd door de leverancier.** At the bottom of the tab, "Bevestigd door de leverancier (laatste 7 dagen): voeg in Exact de bevestiging bij en zet de status op Bevestigd" lists the confirmations the aid prepared to attach, one line each and without a tick, for 7 days after the aid last prepared the attachment or the status for that confirmation, the newest first, with the day it last did so: "18-11-2026: inkooporder 250108 (S08), bestand MU08_conf.pdf". Attach that file to the order in Exact and set its status to Bevestigd. "(alleen bijvoegen: er staat nog iets open)" after a line means that a question or an item of that order is still open: attach the confirmation only; the note goes once the case is closed and the aid has prepared the status, and then you set the status too: the line shows again from the day of the close, also when the confirmation came in more than 7 days before. The confirmation of a try-out (section 3.17) carries "(proefdocument)" behind its line: it is made up, so attach nothing and set no status for it in the real Exact. With no confirmation in those 7 days the section is not shown.

**An order above the authorization limit.** Under the line at the top, the tab names each such order with "Let op: na deze wijziging moet de inkooporder opnieuw gefiatteerd worden.": after you enter the change, the order goes through approval in Exact again. Neither the limit nor the order's total is shown.

**What a tick is.** A tick is your word that the change is in Exact: the aid reads Exact back only through the new PDFs it is sent and the exports from Exact it is given. It is one line in `cases/prepared/marks.jsonl` per prepared row of the change, with the time and "buyer", appended and never rewritten; a change the aid saw has such a line with "version 2" in place of "buyer", or "export" for one it saw in an export ("(gezien in een export uit Exact)"), and "Weer op de lijst zetten" adds a line "reopened". It changes nothing of the case, its write log, its dispositions or `prepared_changes.csv`. "1 regel van de lijst kon niet worden gelezen; vernieuw de lijst over een minuut." means that a line of one of the two files could not be read, usually the last line while the runner is writing it: refresh the list a minute later, and tell the researcher if the line stays. When the list cannot be read at all (another program holds a file of it, for example `prepared_changes.csv` opened in a spreadsheet), the rows stay as they were, the label reads "In Exact invoeren (?)" and the tab says "Dit is niet gelukt (PermissionError)."; close that program and press "Lijst vernieuwen". On a page opened with `--share`, and on a page without its settings file (the Colab notebook), the tab says only that it is not available.

### 2.7 Your settings on the page

Your own rules are on the tab "Instellingen" (Settings) of the review page. You never need to open a settings file for them: everything else was set up at the installation. The tab shows what the settings file holds when the page loads and each time you select the tab, also a change someone made in the file itself; what you typed and did not save is then replaced by the file's values. The page is in Dutch, with the switch "Taal" for English. A number takes a decimal comma or a point, and a percentage or an amount at most two decimals: write a thousand as 1000, because "1.000" is refused with "hoogstens twee decimalen (schrijf duizend als 1000, zonder punt of komma)".

From top to bottom:

- **"Wat de assistent zelf mag afhandelen"** (What the aid may settle by itself).
  - "Prijsverschil: hoogstens dit percentage" and "Prijsverschil: hoogstens dit bedrag per regel (euro)" are the threshold for the price ("de drempel"). When a price difference is at most that percentage of the PO's price, and at most that amount on the line (the difference per piece times the quantity), the aid takes the supplier's price over on the PO line and changes the article's price too (a line without an item code has no article to change). A difference above the threshold comes to you.
  - "Leverdatum: dagen bovenop de levertijd" is the margin for the delivery date ("de marge"). A later delivery date is taken over up to and including the supplier's lead time plus these days, counted from the delivery date of the purchase order's line, not from the day the order was placed. An example: the line asks for delivery on 16 November, the supplier's lead time is 10 days and the margin is 7 days. A confirmed date up to and including 3 December (16 November plus 17 days) is taken over, and 4 December comes to you. A date given as a week counts as its Friday. Every later date of a supplier without a known lead time comes to you. An earlier delivery date is taken over, also without a known lead time.
  - "Eerste zaken van een leverancier": until this many cases of a supplier are closed, every case of that supplier comes to you, whatever it holds, with the reason "Nieuwe leverancier" on the review tab; 0 turns this off.
- **"Vragen aan leveranciers"** (Questions to suppliers). "Wachttijd op een antwoord (werkdagen)": the working days the aid waits for a supplier's answer before the case comes to you. "Uw naam onder de berichten": your name under "Inkoop" in the questions and in the request to the approver, or under "Purchasing" when the aid's language is English; empty leaves "Inkoop" or "Purchasing" alone.
- **"Dagoverzicht en taal"** (Digest and language). "Dagoverzicht": "Elke dag op een vaste tijd", with the time under "Tijd van het dagoverzicht (UU:MM)" (for example 16:30), or "Na elke zaak". "Uw e-mailadres": where the digest goes; you are also in copy of every question and of the request to the approver. "Taal van de assistent", "Nederlands" or "English": the language of the digest, of the messages to suppliers and to the approver, and of the banner in the webshop (the switch "Taal" at the top changes the page only).
- **"Fiattering"** (Approval). "E-mailadres van de fiatteur": the person who is asked to approve a PO again after the aid changed it above the authorization limit. The limit itself is set at the installation and is not shown.

Press "Opslaan" under these groups. The save writes only the fields you changed since the form was filled. A field you did not touch is never written, so a value that was changed in the file meanwhile stays as the file has it, and every other line of the file stays as it was. "Opgeslagen. De assistent gebruikt dit vanaf de volgende ronde, binnen een minuut." means it is saved, and the fields then show the values as the file holds them (a time typed as 9:05 shows as 09:05). With nothing changed the page says "Er is niets gewijzigd; er is niets opgeslagen.", leaves the file alone and shows the file's values as they are at that moment. When a value is not right, nothing is saved and the page names each field with what it takes, for example "Niet opgeslagen: Prijsverschil: hoogstens dit percentage: een getal van 0 tot 100." If the page was started again while your browser tab stayed open, the first save is not made: the page fills the form again with the settings as they are and asks you to enter your change once more.

**What a save changes, and what it does not.** The runner uses the new values from its next pass, the pre-check in the browser from its next check, and the review page itself at once. What was checked before a save keeps its outcome: a case that waits for you keeps its reason, and a cart that was checked before is answered from its case, as section 2.5 describes (the same page on any day, the same cart on the same day). So on the same day a cart is checked anew, with the new values, only when the cart itself changes (another quantity, a line more or less).

**"Leveranciers"** (Suppliers). The table lists every supplier you set something for: the e-mail address for the aid's questions, the lead time in days, "Altijd naar u" (every case of the supplier comes to you) and "Geen vragen uit zichzelf" (the aid sends this supplier no questions by itself). The questions the aid would have asked such a supplier come to you. A question you choose yourself on the review tab, "Leverancier vragen", is still sent to the supplier (section 2.2). Without an address the aid asks the supplier nothing either, and the case comes to you. To change a supplier, choose it under "Leverancier": the form below fills with its values and with the two ticks, "Elke zaak van deze leverancier komt naar u" and "De assistent stuurt deze leverancier uit zichzelf geen vragen". To add one, choose "Leverancier toevoegen" and type its name exactly as the purchase order gives it. Then press "Leverancier opslaan"; of a listed supplier only what you changed is written. "Leverancier verwijderen" takes the chosen supplier out of every list. A listed supplier left without an address, a lead time and both ticks is no longer listed. Two saves are refused. A name typed under "Leverancier toevoegen" that is listed already is not saved, so that a listed supplier never loses its values: "Niet opgeslagen: er staat al een leverancier met deze naam in de lijst. Kies die leverancier bij "Leverancier" om hem te wijzigen." A new supplier with a name and nothing else is not saved either: "Niet opgeslagen: een leverancier heeft een e-mailadres, een levertijd of een vinkje nodig." The note of a supplier save stays on the page until you choose another supplier yourself.

**When the settings file cannot be read.** If the file is missing, held by another program, damaged, or holds a value the aid refuses, one line at the top of the tab says so: "Het instellingenbestand kan niet worden gelezen. Hieronder staan de instellingen die de assistent nu gebruikt. Opslaan kan pas weer als het bestand is hersteld." When a value is refused, the line names its field, by its label on the tab, or by the setting's name for a setting the installation sets, never a value. The form then shows the settings the aid works with at that moment, and every save on the tab is refused with the same line until the file is put right. Tell the researcher.

**"Koppeling met Exact (bij de installatie)"**, folded away below the suppliers, holds the connection to Exact: the layout of the purchase orders that Exact e-mails and the columns of the export of order lines, each set from one sample at the installation, and the words and the digits by which the aid finds the order number on a confirmation (section 3.17). Its lines under "Stand van de koppeling" say whether the layout is set, which purchase order the aid filed last, whether the columns of the export are set, when the last export was filed, how many files in the watched folder it could not read and how many cases wait for their purchase order. You do not need it in daily work.

**"Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst"**, folded away at the bottom of the tab, holds the connection of the mailbox (section 3.14) and of Copilot (section 3.15); a click on its heading opens it. It is set up at the installation. You need it when the digest says "Postvak niet verbonden" or "Copilot werkt niet": the buttons to sign in again are in this part (section 5). At its end stands "Code voor de uitbreiding in de browser": the code to paste into the options of the extension of section 2.5, with a button to copy it; it cannot be changed on the page.

## 3. Running it

### 3.1 The host and the folder layout

The aid reaches the work mailbox through the Microsoft sign-in on the settings tab of the review page (section 3.14), and Exact through the buyer, who enters what the page's tab "In Exact invoeren" lists (section 2.6): no program of the aid reaches Exact, so it needs no Exact token (section 3.10). The first run on documents of the test account needs the company's written permission. What follows is what the code needs, wherever it runs.

The aid runs in one of two ways. From a copy of the repository, with Python 3.11 or newer and its packages installed, as below. Or from a folder that needs no installation at all, built once on a Windows PC and carried to the Windows PC where it runs, where it is started by a double click and at each logon (section 3.16): the way for a PC where nothing can be installed. For the repository, from the folder that holds `aid/`:

```bash

python3 -m pytest aid/tests -q
```

The suite runs offline and calls no AI service.

The folder layout, with the folder settings of the example (`cases_root` "cases", `po_store_root` "po_store", `watched_folder` "inbox"); relative paths in the settings resolve against the folder that holds the settings file. In the folder of section 3.16, `<root>` is its folder `data`:

A save that changes nothing leaves the file and its modification time alone, so that "Opslaan" on an untouched form never changes the hash of the file, which the freeze record keeps (section 4.8). When the file cannot be read (it is missing, held by another program, no JSON, or holds a value the aid refuses), the tab shows one line that says so, naming the fields (a field of the tab by its label, any other setting by its name) and never a value, shows the settings in use, and refuses every save until the file is put right. Set these in the file before the buyer starts; the page never shows them, except the token, which the settings tab shows read-only at the end of its connections, as "Code voor de uitbreiding in de browser", for the extension's options (section 3.13). The runner reads the settings file again at the start of a pass when the file has changed; a file that is not valid then is logged in `runner.log` (stage `settings`, the fields only) and the settings in use are kept. The capture server does the same before each pre-check (its log is `capture.log`), and the review page when its review tab opens or shows a case and before a close or an undo, so that all three work with the same thresholds.

### 3.3 The mailbox rule

Without the Microsoft sign-in of section 3.14, a mailbox rule fills `inbox/`; it is set up at the installation. It must:

- save the e-mails that carry Exact's PO PDFs, so that the aid knows every PO version;
- save the suppliers' e-mails with their attachments (confirmations, and any other document);
- save the replies to the aid's questions;
- save each as a whole e-mail, an `.eml` file, straight into `inbox/` (not into a subfolder).

Whole e-mails are best: the aid then reads the PO number from the subject and body too, and it can file a reply only by its headers. A `.pdf` or `.png` saved on its own is also handled, and so is an export of the order lines (`.xlsx` or `.csv`) once `export_columns` is set (section 3.13, which also says how another spreadsheet is skipped then); any other file is skipped as "unsupported file type". In the Bachelor End Project the documents of the test-account cases are to be re-sent to the researcher's company mailbox, and the aid reaches that mailbox through the Microsoft sign-in on the settings tab (section 3.14) instead of through a rule.

Instead of this rule, the runner can read a folder of the work mailbox itself after a Microsoft sign-in (section 3.14); it saves the e-mails into `inbox/` in the same form.

**Purchase orders that are already under way.** The aid knows a purchase order from Exact's e-mail with its PDF (or from an export, section 3.13). A confirmation that names an order the aid does not know waits for it: the confirmations of the first weeks, which answer orders sent before the connection, and a webshop's confirmation that comes before Exact's e-mail. Such a case is checked again by itself once the order comes in (section 3.17, "A confirmation that comes before its purchase order"). So at hand-over, drag the e-mails with Exact's PO PDFs of the last weeks into the folder of the mailbox that the aid reads. An e-mail that lay in that folder before the mailbox was connected is left alone (section 3.14): drag it out of the folder and in again. Or save the PDFs into `inbox/`. At its next pass the runner files the orders and checks the cases that wait for them again.

### 3.4 Starting the runner and keeping it running

```bash
python3 -m aid.runner --settings <root>/settings.json [--poll-seconds N] [--max-passes N]
```

- `--settings` (required): the settings file. The STOP file is looked for next to it.
- `--poll-seconds`: seconds between passes, default 60.
- `--max-passes`: stop after this many passes; leave it out in production.

At its start the runner empties `cases/tmp/work` (section 3.1), so that a copy left by a runner that was ended in the middle of it never stays, and removes from the watched folder the copies that a file given on the review page left there when its copy was cut off (names that begin with `.dropin_` and end with `.part`, which the scan never reads), once they are older than ten minutes; a copy that cannot be removed is one line in `runner.log`, and the runner goes on. Each pass: handle the inbox, run every new case, end the waits that are over, and make the digest when it is due. The command runs until the STOP file exists, then prints the number of passes and ends with exit code 0. It ends at once with exit code 2 when the settings file cannot be read or is not valid, when `reader` is `"llm"` without `ms_tenant_id` and `ms_client_id`, or when the writer refuses the Exact settings (for example an address that is not https).

How it is kept running (a scheduled task at start-up, or a service) is the operator's choice on the host. Whatever starts it must:

- run it from the folder that holds `aid/`, with Python 3.11 or newer and the packages installed (or from the folder of section 3.16, whose start files do all of this);
- give the full path of the settings file;
- put `AID_EXACT_TOKEN` (when `exact_base_url` is set) and `AID_MAIL_PASSWORD` (when `smtp_host` and `smtp_user` are set) in the environment of the process, from the company's secret store, before the start;
- start it again if it ends with a code other than 0;
- with the work mailbox connected (section 3.14) or with `"reader": "llm"` (Copilot, section 3.15), run it as the same Windows user who signs in on the review page, not as a service account or SYSTEM: the sign-in's tokens are encrypted for that user, and the mailbox and Copilot use the same tokens;
- run one runner per `<root>`, never two.

The runner uses the host's clock, so `digest_time` is the host's local time.

#### 3.4.1 One launcher for the three parts

On the buyer's own PC, the runner, the review page (section 3.7) and the capture server (section 3.13) run side by side. One command starts the three and keeps them running:

```bash
python -m aid.start --settings <root>/settings.json [--testshop] [--poll-seconds N] [--background]
python -m aid.start --settings <root>/settings.json --status
python -m aid.start --settings <root>/settings.json --stop
python -m aid.start --settings <root>/settings.json --resume [--testshop] [--poll-seconds N] [--background]
python -m aid.start --settings <root>/settings.json [--testshop] [--poll-seconds N] --install-autostart
python -m aid.start --settings <root>/settings.json --remove-autostart
```

- **What it starts.** `python -m aid.runner` (with `--poll-seconds` when given), `python -m aid.review` and `python -m aid.capture_server` (with `--testshop` when given), each with the settings file by its full path, from the folder that holds `aid/`, and with the same Python that runs the launcher. Each part is started with `-X utf8 -u` before `-m`, so that its output file holds every line at once and in UTF-8 also under a Python that does not read the environment variables `PYTHONIOENCODING` and `PYTHONUNBUFFERED`, which the launcher sets as well. A launcher that runs isolated (`-I`, as every Python of the folder of section 3.16 does) starts its parts with `-I` too, and so does its start at logon: an isolated Python reads none of Python's environment variables and leaves the user's own folder of packages out. On Windows the word `python` may be a placeholder that does nothing; then call the launcher with the full path of the Python (for example `C:\Users\<you>\AppData\Local\Programs\Python\Python311\python.exe`), and the parts and the start at logon use that path too. The parts open no window. The settings file is read first: when it cannot be read, the launcher names the file and the fields at fault, never their values, starts nothing and ends with exit code 2.
- **In a window or in the background.** A launcher started with the first command lives in the window it was typed in, and its first line says so: closing that window stops the three parts. With `--background` the command first checks, in the window, whether the start is possible (the settings, `STOP`, a launcher that already runs, parts that already answer) and says why when it is not. Otherwise it starts the launcher apart from the window (on Windows with `pythonw.exe`, so without a window of its own), prints where the logs are and returns; the window can then be closed. `--resume --background` does the same after deleting `STOP`. The launcher that Windows starts at logon is such a launcher without a window.
- **The parts end with the launcher.** On Windows the launcher ties the three parts to itself with a job object of Windows: when the launcher ends in any way, also when its window is closed, when it is ended in Task Manager or at a logoff, Windows ends the parts with it. What a part starts itself, such as the browser window that the review page opens for the Microsoft sign-in, is not tied and stays open. If Windows refuses the tie, `start.log` says once "the parts could not be tied to the launcher" and the launcher goes on; only then can parts stay behind when the launcher is ended from outside. On Linux and macOS there is no such tie: the launcher stops its parts on Ctrl-C, on Ctrl-\, on SIGTERM and when its terminal is closed, but not when it is killed outright.
- **One launcher on a PC.** The review page and the capture server always take http://127.0.0.1:7860/ and http://127.0.0.1:7870/health, so only one launcher runs on a PC, whatever its settings folder. Before it starts anything, a launcher asks both addresses. When one of them answers, it starts nothing, ends with exit code 3 and writes "something already answers at <address>: only one launcher runs on a PC; a launcher on other settings is stopped with --stop on its own settings, and parts started by hand are closed". A launcher that runs on another settings folder (the try-out folder `live`, for example) is stopped with `--stop` on that folder's settings file, not by closing its parts, which it would start again; a review page or a capture server started by hand is closed in its own window. When the state file of the last launcher on these settings still names parts as running that were not tied to it (on Linux and macOS, or after a failed tie), a second line gives their names and process ids: close those by the process id (Task Manager, Details). `--stop` on settings where no launcher runs names each address that still answers in the same words, because `STOP` ends a runner only.
- **The logs.** Everything goes to the folder `logs/` next to the settings file: `runner.out`, `review.out` and `capture.out` hold what each part prints (appended, never emptied), and `start.log` holds the launcher's own lines with the time: each start, each end with its exit code, each start again, a part given up, the stop. A fault of the launcher itself is written to `start.log` with its traceback, because a launcher without a window has no other place to show it. The runner's own `cases/runner.log` and the capture server's `cases/capture.log` stay where section 3.6 says.
- **The restart rule.** The launcher looks at the parts every second. A part that ends is started again after 5 seconds, at most 5 times in any 10 minutes; after that it is given up and the other two keep running. A part that ends with exit code 2 had its settings refused (for example the capture server with an empty `capture_token`): `start.log` says so and names its `.out` file, which holds the reason. Such a part is started again as soon as the settings file changes, for example after "Opslaan" on the settings tab, and that start is not one of the five. A part that cannot be started at all is logged with the error itself. When all three have ended or been given up, the launcher ends with exit code 1.
- **The STOP file is respected.** While `STOP` exists next to the settings (section 3.5), the launcher starts nothing, writes "stopped by the STOP file: delete it, or use --resume" and ends with exit code 3; this holds at logon too. When `STOP` appears while it runs, it ends the review page and the capture server at once, lets the runner end by itself at its next pass (and ends it after its poll time plus 30 seconds if it has not), and ends with exit code 0. Ctrl-C in its window, SIGTERM and, on Linux and macOS, Ctrl-\ and a closed terminal stop it the same way, within a second: the launcher then creates `STOP` itself for the time of the stop, with the text "launcher" and the time, so that the runner ends by itself, and deletes the file again when the parts have ended. A `STOP` file that was already there is never changed or deleted by such a stop. A second Ctrl-C, a second or more after the first, ends the parts at once. If such a stop is cut off, for example because the PC is switched off, the launcher's own `STOP` file can stay behind; the next start sees by its text that no person made it, removes it and says so. `--stop` creates `STOP`, says that it waits, and waits up to 90 seconds for the three to end, then says whether they did. `--resume` deletes `STOP` and starts as above. When a launcher is still stopping, `--resume` first waits for it to end, at most 150 seconds, and only then deletes `STOP` and starts; when that launcher has not ended by then, it starts nothing and leaves `STOP` as it is, and when another launcher was started meanwhile (by a second `--resume`), it starts nothing either. A Ctrl-C while `--stop` waits ends that command and leaves `STOP` in place, so the launcher goes on stopping; a Ctrl-C during the watch of a start (below) or the wait of `--resume` ends that command, and nothing is started. Both say so in one line and end with exit code 130.
- **The state file, and a launcher that ended without a stop.** The launcher keeps `start_state.json` next to the settings: its start, a heartbeat written every 5 seconds, the runner's poll time, and per part its start, the number of starts, whether it was given up, whether it was tied to the launcher and, while it runs, its process id. A second launcher on the same settings is refused. A start that finds a heartbeat older than 20 seconds takes the file for the leftover of a launcher that ended without a stop (a crash, a shutdown, a closed window). A younger heartbeat is watched for 7 seconds, and the window says so first. When it moves, a launcher runs: the start says "already running" and ends with exit code 3. When it has not moved, the file is a leftover as well. So after a quick log off and on, when the heartbeat of the session before is still fresh, the launcher at logon starts 7 seconds later instead of not at all. When the leftover names a runner that was not tied to its launcher (Linux and macOS, or a failed tie on Windows), that runner may still be running, and only `STOP` ends it: the start makes the launcher's own `STOP` file, says that it gives that runner its poll time plus 5 seconds to end (65 seconds when the launcher was started without `--poll-seconds`), waits that long, removes the file and goes on. `--background` and `--install-autostart` make this watch and this wait in the window and remove the leftover there ("found the state file (start_state.json) of a launcher that no longer runs: it is removed"), so that the launcher they start does neither again; a launcher in a window or at logon replaces the file itself. A start after a leftover thus takes up to 7 seconds longer, and where the parts were not tied also the runner's poll time plus 5 seconds. `--status` prints one line for the launcher and one per part, and whether the review page (http://127.0.0.1:7860/) and the capture server (http://127.0.0.1:7870/health) answer; it ends with 0 while the launcher runs and with 1 otherwise. For up to 20 seconds after a launcher ended without a stop, it still shows that launcher as running. After that, on Windows, it shows "launcher: not running; it was ended from outside (last heartbeat ...), and its parts ended with it" and each part as "ended with the launcher": Windows ended them, and there is nothing to close. Where the parts were not tied, it shows "it ended without stopping its parts", the runner as "may still run (process N); it obeys the STOP file", and a review page or a capture server as "leftover, process N" while its address answers and as "ended" otherwise. The next start replaces the file. The status lines and the addresses assume the default ports: a review page that moved to another port (section 3.7) shows as not answering.
- **The start at logon on Windows.** `--install-autostart` puts a shortcut `Inkoopassistent.lnk` in the user's own Startup folder (normally `%APPDATA%\Microsoft\Windows\Start Menu\Programs\Startup`; a Start Menu that the organisation moved elsewhere is followed), which starts the launcher with `pythonw.exe` beside the Python in use, so that no window opens, with the same settings, `--testshop` and `--poll-seconds` as given. It needs no administrator rights. The shortcut is made through PowerShell and read back. When PowerShell cannot be used, or the saved shortcut does not hold the paths it was given (a path with a character that the shortcut cannot keep), a file `Inkoopassistent.cmd` in the same folder does the same, and the command says which of the two it made. When neither can be made, the command says that the start at logon could not be set, prints what the shortcut would run (so that one can be made by hand in the Startup folder), starts nothing and ends with exit code 1. Otherwise it starts the launcher in the background at once, after the same checks as any start, and prints where the logs are. Its last line asks you to log off and on once and to check with `--status` that the launcher started, after `--resume --background` when `STOP` kept the launcher from starting now: that is the only real test of the start at logon. From then on the three parts start by themselves each time this user logs on. The shortcut holds the full paths of the Python, of the folder that holds `aid/` and of the settings file. When the project folder or the settings folder is moved, or the Python is installed elsewhere, run `--install-autostart` again: otherwise nothing starts at logon and nothing is logged. To take it away, run `--remove-autostart`, which deletes the shortcut and the `.cmd` file; a launcher that is running goes on until `--stop`. On another system both options say that they are for Windows and end with exit code 2.
- **Environment variables.** The parts get the environment of the launcher. A variable set in one PowerShell window (`$env:AID_EXACT_TOKEN = "..."`) reaches a launcher started from that window, also with `--background` or `--install-autostart`, but not the launcher that Windows starts at the next logon. A variable the aid needs (`AID_EXACT_TOKEN`, `AID_MAIL_PASSWORD`, section 3.4) must therefore be a user variable of Windows ("Edit environment variables for your account" in the Start menu), so that the launcher at logon has it too. Without the token every write to Exact is refused and prepared instead (section 5, "A write was refused"), and without the mailbox password every message is written as a file, without a warning (section 3.11).

The exit codes of the launcher: 0 a clean stop or a done action, also `--install-autostart` once the start at logon is set, whatever became of the start now, and `--stop` whatever became of the stop; 1 every part ended, a fault of the launcher, a start at logon that could not be set, `--status` when no launcher runs, a start in the background that cannot be made, or `--remove-autostart` that cannot delete a file; 2 the settings or an option refused, also a settings file that does not exist; 3 not started (`STOP` exists, a launcher already runs, a part already answers, another launcher was started on the same settings in the same moment, or `--resume` found that the launcher it waited for has not ended or that another one was started meanwhile); 130 a Ctrl-C outside the supervision (the watch of a start, the waits of `--stop` and `--resume`).

### 3.5 The STOP file

Create an empty file `STOP` next to the settings file. The runner ends before its next pass; a pass that is running finishes first, and the runner may take up to the poll interval (60 seconds by default) to notice the file. While `STOP` exists, a new start makes no pass at all. Delete `STOP` before starting the runner again. Documents that arrive meanwhile wait in the inbox and are handled once the runner runs again. `STOP` does not stop a review page started on its own. The launcher of section 3.4.1 obeys `STOP` as well: it starts nothing while the file exists, and when the file appears it stops all three parts, the review page and the capture server included. A `STOP` file whose text begins with the word "launcher" is the launcher's own, made for the time of a stop that it started itself (Ctrl-C, for example), or by a start for the time it gives a runner left behind by an earlier launcher to end, and deleted afterwards; leave such a file alone.

### 3.6 The logs and the audit record

`cases/runner.log` has one line per problem: the time, the case id or the stage (`scan`, `scan <file>`, `recheck`, `recheck <case>` for a confirmation whose check again failed (section 3.17), `list of changes` for the comparison of the list "In Exact invoeren" with the orders as they stand (section 2.6), `escalation`, `digest`, `cases root`, `digest_state.json`, `mailbox`, `settings`, `work folder`, `watched folder`), and the error. A case that keeps failing is logged once per run of the loop, and once more when it later succeeds. The pass goes on after an error.

`cases/capture.log` is the capture server's: one line per refused request (the status and the reason; a page without a cart table names its kept copy `captures/nocart_<...>`), per error outside a case's run (with its traceback; the banner shows "Voorcontrole mislukt" with "the pre-check failed; see capture.log") and per connection that ended badly (a client that stalled past the 60-second timeout, a request the server could not parse). A case whose run failed is not in it: its error is in the case folder, as for any failed case (section 5). The token is never in it. The server also prints one line per capture on its console: the time, the shop's host, the case id and the verdict.

`cases/digest_state.json` holds `last_sent` (the time of the last digest, or null), `reported` (the cases the next digest reports), `skipped` (the skipped files it lists), `mailbox_lapsed` while the Microsoft sign-in has lapsed (section 5, "Postvak niet verbonden"), `copilot` while Copilot fails as a whole (the error and the number of cases waiting for it), and `copilot_cases` while Copilot has failed a case (the cases, with their refusals and failures, in the order they are tried last; section 3.15). When it cannot be read, a fresh one is started and the log says so.

`cases/<case>/audit.json` is the audit record, for the researcher, IT and the thesis; the buyer never sees it. It holds the inputs by sha256, the model (none in parser mode), the settings with the authorization limit only as "set" or "unset", every step's output with its seconds, every rule decision with the settings it read, every write and message, the errors, and the timings. The review page adds a list `close`, the mailbox a list `answers`, and the escalation a list `escalations`.

### 3.7 The review page on the host

```bash
python3 -m aid.review --settings <root>/settings.json [--share]
```

It runs on its own, beside the runner, and serves the page on 127.0.0.1 of the host only: open it in a browser on that machine. The page answers its own host only: a request whose Host header is not 127.0.0.1 or localhost, alone or with the page's port, gets "Invalid host header" (status 400) and reaches nothing of the page, so that a web page whose domain name points at this computer cannot press the page's buttons. Open the page by one of those two names. It uses port 7860 when that port is free. If it is taken (for example by a review page that is still running), the page starts on the next free port, up to 7959, and prints its address. Stop the old page first, or set `review_url` to the printed address, so that the digest's links work. `--share` opens a public link and is for the made-up set only: with company settings (an Exact address, endpoints or fields, an authorization limit, an SMTP server or login, or an address outside example.com and example.org) it is refused with exit code 2. An unreadable settings file also gives exit code 2. The page has three tabs: "Beoordelen", the review of the cases, "In Exact invoeren", the changes to enter in Exact (section 2.6), and "Instellingen", the buyer's settings and suppliers (section 2.7) and, folded away at its bottom under "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst", the connection of the work mailbox (section 3.14) and of the AI service (section 3.15), which writes into the settings file the page was started with. With `--share` the host of the public link passes the check of the Host header as well. On a page opened with `--share` the tab "In Exact invoeren" and the settings tab say only "Niet beschikbaar op een openbare link" and offer nothing, and `--share` is refused altogether while the settings name a tenant (`ms_tenant_id`) or a folder of the work mailbox (`mail_folder`), since the cases may then come from company e-mails.

The page keeps a file uploaded to it and the page images of a document it shows as temporary files in `cases/tmp/` under `cases_root` (the page library's temporary folder, `GRADIO_TEMP_DIR`, which the command sets before it loads the library). While it runs, it removes a temporary file between one and two hours after it was made: once an hour it removes those older than an hour. It removes them all at its next start, when it empties that folder, all but its folder `work`, where the runner keeps a copy for a moment (section 3.1). When it ends, it removes them only at a stop with Ctrl-C in its own window: a page that is ended from outside, as the launcher of section 3.4.1 ends it at a stop, leaves them until its next start. So no document of a case lies outside the aid's own folders. The page also tells the browser to keep none of these files (`Cache-Control: no-store` on each), so that the page images of a document do not stay in the browser's cache, which lies outside them too. When the environment names a folder in `GRADIO_TEMP_DIR` already, the page uses that folder and does not empty it.

As the command builds the page, its writer is the prepared writer and its mailer writes files: a close prepares its changes in `cases/prepared/prepared_changes.csv`, where the tab "In Exact invoeren" lists them for the buyer (section 2.6), and writes its questions as `.eml` files in `cases/outbox/`, even when the runner itself sends by SMTP or through the work mailbox. Wiring the SMTP mailer into the page is not built yet; until then the questions a close writes as files are sent by hand.

### 3.8 Where the digest goes

The runner always writes the HTML version to `cases/digests/<YYYY-MM-DD>.html` (a second digest on the same day replaces it). The text goes to `buyer_email` the way `send_via` says (section 3.2): through the work mailbox while its sign-in works (section 3.14), through the aid's mailbox when SMTP is set up, and otherwise as an `.eml` file into `cases/outbox/`. A digest that the work mailbox cannot send because the sign-in has lapsed is written into `cases/outbox/` too, and counts as made.

### 3.9 Teams via Power Automate

A Power Automate flow outside the code can relay the digest to the buyer in Teams, so that no AI service is connected to Teams. The runner sets up no flow.

- Trigger: "When a new email arrives" (Office 365 Outlook connector) in the mailbox that receives the digest, with a subject that starts with `[Inkoopassistent]` and the sender the digest really has: `aid_email` when the aid sends through SMTP, the address of the work mailbox when it sends through that mailbox (section 3.14; with `mailbox` empty, the signed-in account's own address). The prefix is the same in both languages. A digest written into `cases/outbox/` is not sent, and reaches no flow.

If the flow is not allowed, the e-mail is the notification.

### 3.10 How changes reach Exact

**The list.** The aid writes nothing in Exact. The buyer opens Exact Globe+ as a virtual application, which no program of the aid reaches, so every change is prepared: the prepared writer adds a row to `cases/prepared/prepared_changes.csv` (columns `entry_id`, `case_id`, `po_number`, `version`, `line_id`, `article_code`, `kind`, `old_value`, `new_value`, `document`, `rule_id`, `timestamp`), and the buyer enters the change in Exact from the tab "In Exact invoeren" of the review page, which keeps his ticks in `cases/prepared/marks.jsonl` beside it (section 2.6). Only a price, a delivery date or a quantity of an order line and an article's purchase price are rows of the list. The aid prepares the confirmation to attach and the status Bevestigd too, which are no rows to tick: the tab shows each confirmation at its bottom for 7 days after the aid last prepared the attachment or the status, the digest marks them "(zelf in Exact doen)", and the buyer attaches the confirmation and sets the status Bevestigd in Exact himself, as he does today. The runner, the review page and the batch prepare into that folder; give the price-list import the same folder (section 4.3). Nobody applies the rows from the file itself. A tick given by mistake is taken back on the tab with "Weer op de lijst zetten" (section 2.6). By hand, the same is `reopen(Path("<root>/cases/prepared"), ["<entry id>", ...], datetime.now())` of `aid/execution/by_hand.py`, the entry ids being those of the change's rows in `prepared_changes.csv`: it writes the mark "reopened" only when that puts the change on the list again, and returns the state the change would have otherwise; for a change that was put back, by the page or with the recipe below once its undo is entered or seen, it writes nothing and returns "undone". Do not use `mark(..., "reopened")` for this: it writes the mark whatever the state, so that while a put-back of the change waits on the list, the change and its put-back cancel each other and both leave the list, while Exact still holds the entered value.

**`exact_base_url` stays empty.** So do `exact_endpoints` and `exact_fields`. The Exact writer behind them (`aid/execution/exact_api.py`), built and tested against a fake server, reads a field through a web API, writes the new value and reads it back; that fits no door of Exact Globe+, so it is not set at the installation. If it were set, the runner would write through it, with the prepared writer behind it; a case made from a try-out confirmation (section 3.17) would still write through the prepared writer only, never through the Exact writer.

**What a program door would need.** Of the three ways in that Exact names for a program, the one a writer of the aid could use is the Entity Services of Exact. The company would have to open: the Entity Services of Exact on a company server that the aid's PC reaches; an Exact user for the aid with rights on purchase orders (and on articles, for a purchase price); the name of that server and of the database (the administration); and one point is open, whether the Entity Services let a line of an existing purchase order be updated at all.

**Where it would plug in.** A writer for that door implements the Writer protocol of `aid/execution/writer.py` and stands in front of the prepared writer, as `RetryingWriter` puts the Exact writer in front of it today: `build_deps` of the runner would make `RetryingWriter(<the new writer>, PreparedWriter(<cases_root>/prepared))`. A change that writer makes is an entry made in the system (status "done"): it is in the case's write log with its old value, has an undo link in the digest and is offered for undo on the tab "Beoordelen", and it never reaches `prepared_changes.csv`, so it never reaches the list. A change it cannot make falls back to the prepared writer: it lands on the list, and the case comes to the review page with "prepared change: apply by hand".

**The undo.** The undo applies the reverse change (the old value back; an attached file removed) and logs it with `undo_of` naming the change. Each change is undone once, but for "Terugdraaien in Exact" on the tab, which undoes a change a second time once its first put-back was replaced or dropped and nothing newer stands in the way (section 2.6). A change that was only prepared was not made, so the review page offers no undo for it: the buyer drops it on the tab "In Exact invoeren" instead. From the review page an undo is prepared too (section 3.7), and so lands on the list; the page then says "Nog niet teruggedraaid in Exact. Op de lijst staat nu: ...", never "Ongedaan gemaakt". To undo every change of a case through the runner's writer, newest first:

```python
import os
from datetime import datetime
from pathlib import Path
from aid.settings import load_settings
from aid.runner import build_deps
from aid.execution.writer import undo_case

settings = load_settings(Path("<root>/settings.json"))
deps = build_deps(settings, os.environ)
print(undo_case(settings.cases_root / "<case id>", deps.writer, datetime.now()))
```

On the list (section 2.6) an undo counts as follows. It belongs to the last change of its place (the order line, or the article) whose new value is the value it takes away, and it takes away only its own case's request in that change. When that request was never ticked as entered, the undo takes it off the list, and itself too, as if neither had been asked: Exact still holds the value from before. When another case asked the same value, the change stays on the list for that case alone, and ticking it later brings back nothing of what the undo took away. When the request was ticked as entered, the undo is a change to enter of its own, the old value back: "Inkooporder 250108 (S08), regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,70 → 12,50", or, for an article price whose price before it the aid never read, "artikel KM-0801: inkoopprijs 12,70 terugzetten naar de prijs van daarvoor", where the buyer puts back the price that Exact held before. An undo whose case asked nothing in that change takes nothing away: it is a change to enter when that change was ticked as entered, and is left out otherwise. The recipe has its limits: an undo made with it that a later case's request for the same value joins, or whose change's tick is taken back, may be passed over by the list; prefer "Terugdraaien in Exact" on the tab (section 2.6).

**Seen in a new purchase order.** No program reads Exact, but Exact e-mails a new PDF of the purchase order when a changed order is approved and released again (process record, steps 5 and 6; whether the test Exact does so is the owner's check A7). At each pass, after the cases ran, the runner compares every open change of an order line with the order as it stands (`reconcile` of `aid/execution/by_hand.py`: `PoStore.as_it_stands`, the newest PDF version or an export filed after it) and marks a change whose new value that look shows as seen, with a line in `marks.jsonl` whose "by" is "version <n>", or "export" for an export; the rules are in section 2.6. The look counts only when it was filed after the newest row of the change was prepared (`PoStore.filed_at` against the row's `timestamp` in `prepared_changes.csv`): the version the case compared with never ticks a change off, and a put-back is ticked off only by a print or an export filed after the put-back itself. The time that counts is when the aid filed the look, not when Exact made it: a print that reaches the aid late (the runner was stopped, the sign-in of the work mailbox had lapsed, or a PDF was saved and put in the watched folder later) counts as new, and can tick a change or a put-back off although Exact printed it before the buyer entered the change; section 2.6 asks the buyer to check those put-backs in Exact. An article price is never seen in a purchase order; a change that is entered, seen or dropped stays so whatever a later PDF shows, and a second pass over the same documents marks nothing more. An error of this step is logged in `runner.log` as "list of changes", once in a run of the runner, and the pass goes on. The digest counts the changes still open after it.

**Put back from the tab.** "Terugdraaien in Exact" (section 2.6) is the undo of one change that is in Exact, for the buyer: for a change of a case it calls the undo of the review page's close (`undo` of `aid/review/close.py`, as above), so that the case's write log, `steps/09_close.json` and audit record hold it; for an article price of a price list it calls `undo_entry` on the list's write-log folder, the folder under `<cases_root>/pricelists/` whose `write_log.jsonl` holds the change (the import's default folder with `--settings`). A list imported with `--write-log` elsewhere, an article price whose price before it the aid never read, and an undo itself cannot be put back from the tab, and the page says so. The undo is prepared through the page's writer, so it is a change to enter, the old value back, as above; one more line of `marks.jsonl`, `{"entry_id": <the new row>, "mark": "puts_back", "of": <the change's entry>, "at": ...}`, links it to the change, so that it shows with "Terugzetten:" before it and a second press does not prepare it again while that put-back is on the list, entered or seen. A press undoes a change a second time once its first put-back was replaced or dropped and nothing newer stands in the way: a second undo entry with the same `undo_of` in the write log, and a second "undo" record in `steps/09_close.json`. A change undone before with the recipe above is linked to that undo instead.

### 3.11 The SMTP mailer

Set `smtp_host`, `smtp_port` (587) and `smtp_user`, and put the password in `AID_MAIL_PASSWORD` before the runner starts. The runner checks for the variable once, at the start: without it, it writes every message as a file into `cases/outbox/` and sends nothing, without a warning. The mailer uses STARTTLS and checks the server's certificate. It sends from `aid_email`. An error names its kind, never the password, the login or a recipient. Each message carries a Message-ID, by which a reply is filed. In the Bachelor End Project the questions go to the researcher's address only, never to a real supplier: `supplier_emails` holds that address.

### 3.12 Reading without AI: the parser

The runner reads with Microsoft 365 Copilot (`"reader": "llm"`, section 3.15) or with the template parser (`"reader": "parser"`). Company documents never go to another AI service. In parser mode:

- a document without a text layer (a scan, a photo) cannot be classified: it is skipped as "no text layer; class unknown" and listed in the digest;
- a document with text but no known keyword is skipped as "other";
- a confirmation is read only if a parser template fits its layout (section 4.1); otherwise the case fails.

Not built yet: the templates of the real suppliers' layouts (Task 5b).

**The PO PDF.** A PO PDF is read without AI under either reader. Without a layout in the settings the PO reader knows only the made-up layout. The layout of the PO PDF that Exact e-mails is the setting `po_layout`: it is written on the company side, from one of the company's own POs, in words that stand on the page, so no PO of the company is ever needed in the repository. The settings tab of the review page writes it from one sample PO, after a trial read of that sample (section 3.17). Its keys:

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `po_label` | The text that stands before the PO number, on the same line: one word, or several words matched as a phrase ("Inkooporder nr."). Required. |
| `date_label`, `version_label`, `currency_label`, `incoterm_label` | The text before the PO's date, its version number, its currency and its delivery terms. Leave a key out, or give `null`, when the PDF does not print the value. Without a version label the versions of a PO are numbered in the order they arrive. |
| `supplier_label` or `supplier_line` | The text before the supplier's name; or, when the name stands alone on a line, the number of that line on page 1, counted from 1 from the top. One of the two, or neither. |
| `title_row` | The words of the row of column titles above the order lines, from left to right, as printed, one word per entry: a title of two words is two entries ("Gewenste", "levering"). |
| `columns` | What each of those words is the title of, one entry per word: `line_id`, `item_code`, `description`, `quantity`, `unit`, `unit_price`, `price_basis`, `discount_pct`, `net_price` or `delivery_date`; `same` for a word that belongs to the title on its left (the second word of "Gewenste levering"); `skip` for a column the aid does not read. One column must be `item_code`, and one must be `line_id` or `quantity`. |
| `table_end` | Optional: a text that stands only on the row after the last order line, best the whole label of the total row ("Totaal exclusief btw"). It is matched as whole words, case not counting, and only on a row that is no order line. That row ends the table on its page, and a later page is read only when it shows the row of column titles again. A page subtotal that holds the text ends that page's table. |
| `decimal_comma` | `true`, the default, for numbers written as 1.234,50; `false` for 1,234.50. |
| `prices_per_piece` | `true` when the PDF has no column for what a price is per and every price is per one unit. The lines then still hold no price basis, so that a confirmation that prints none is compared with them, and only the PO's total, which is held against the authorization limit, counts each line that prints no basis as per one. With `false`, the default, the total of a PO without price bases is unknown, and so it is when a basis is printed and cannot be read. The total is worked out when the PO is filed. |

A layout is refused as any setting at fault is (section 3.2), by its key and never by a word it holds, when: `po_label` is empty; `title_row` and `columns` differ in length or have fewer than two entries; an entry of `title_row` is empty or holds a space (each printed word is an entry of its own, and the second word of a title gets `same`); the first column is `same`; no column is `item_code`; a field stands twice in `columns` (`same` and `skip` may stand as often as needed); there is neither a `line_id` nor a `quantity` column; both `supplier_label` and `supplier_line` are given; or a key is not one of the keys above.

The made-up PO `aid/madeup/tryout/ANDERS_po_v1.pdf` is in a second made-up layout, for trying this out. It is read with:

```json
"po_layout": {
  "po_label": "Bestelnummer",
  "date_label": "Besteldatum",
  "supplier_line": 3,
  "currency_label": "Valuta",
  "incoterm_label": "Leveringsconditie",
  "title_row": ["Hoeveelheid", "Eenh.", "Artikelcode", "Benaming", "Gewenste", "levering", "Stuksprijs", "Bedrag"],
  "columns": ["quantity", "unit", "item_code", "description", "delivery_date", "same", "unit_price", "skip"],
  "decimal_comma": true,
  "prices_per_piece": true
}
```

**How a PO PDF is read with the layout.** A label is found as a word or a phrase of its own, in any case: "Inkooporder" is not found in "Inkoopordernummer", and a label of several words is matched as a whole. A label that stands inside a longer label of the layout at the same place is passed by: with the labels "Order" and "Order date", the line "Order date: 03-11-2026" holds the date label and no PO label. A header value is the text after its label, without the colon, hash or full stop after the label, and up to the next label of the layout on that line, so two labels may share a line. The PO number is the first word of that text when it holds a digit, the version when it is a whole number, and the date when it reads as a date; the PO number and the version lose a comma, semicolon, colon or full stop printed right after them ("Inkooporder 250101," gives 250101). A line where that word is none of these gives nothing, and the next line that holds the label is tried. So a title "Inkooporder  Pagina 1 van 1" above "Inkooporder: 26001234" gives the number 26001234. The supplier, the currency and the delivery terms are the whole text after the first line that holds their label.

Below the row of column titles a row is an order line when, with a line number column, its line number is a whole number and its quantity or its price reads as a number (either is enough), or, in a layout without line numbers, when it has an item code and its quantity is a number; the lines are then numbered 1, 2, 3. So a total row, the second row of a long description and a footer that begins with a postal code are no lines and are not read. A page that does not repeat the column titles continues the table; a page that repeats them is cut by its own titles, and its rows above them are no lines. A number is read with its column also when it is printed flush right under a short title: the word that stands under the title takes the column, and a first word left of the title goes with it when the two are one number printed one space apart, with a space before its thousands ("1 234,50") or with a currency sign before it ("€ 4,20"). A number with a space as its thousands mark whose first part stands under the title on its left is read short: "12 345,00" with its "12" under the end of the title "Eenh." gives the price 345,00 and the unit "st 12". The trial read on the page (section 3.17) shows it. Two cells that the PDF prints close together, as a price that ends 2 points before its basis, are read as two cells: a word of the text layer is cut where the gap between two of its characters is wider than the spacing of its letters. A PDF in which the PO label is followed by two different PO numbers, as a batch print of two POs, is refused; a page header that repeats the same number on every page is fine.

**Which PDF is a PO.** With `po_layout` set, a PDF fits the layout when its first page holds the whole row of column titles, word for word, and the PO label with a value after it. A PDF that fits is filed as a PO whatever else its first page says below its column titles, so a PO that asks its supplier for an "orderbevestiging" under its lines is still a PO, and it is filed also when a case already holds the same file. Two exceptions keep a supplier's document out of the PO store. A PDF whose header, the lines of its first page above the column titles, holds a confirmation word anywhere ("Orderbevestiging", "Order confirmation", "Purchase order confirmation", "Auftragsbestätigung" and the like) is classed by the keywords as before, although it fits: so is a confirmation whose title stands below the supplier's letterhead, and so is a PO of the company whose header holds such a word, which is then taken for a confirmation. And when `po_senders` is filled, a PDF that fits and came in an e-mail from a sender that is not on the list is handled as any confirmation is, also when its title says it is a PO: a supplier may answer with the PO itself. A sender is on the list by its address or by its domain; a file put into `inbox/` on its own has no sender and passes. A PDF that does not fit is classed by the keywords as before. What is left: without `po_senders`, or from a listed sender, a supplier's document that repeats the PO's column titles and its label and has no confirmation word in its header is filed as a version of the PO, unless it prints a version number that the store already holds from another file, which the store refuses. So fill `po_senders` whenever `po_layout` is set.

With a layout set the made-up layout is no longer read: a PO PDF is read with the layout or refused. A PO PDF that is refused stays in `inbox/` and is listed in the digest under "Overgeslagen" with its reason: "does not fit the purchase order layout" (the first page lacks the row of column titles, or the PO label with a value after it), "no PO number found" (on no line is the PO label followed by a word with a digit), "more than one PO number" (two different numbers after the PO label), "cannot be the name of a folder" (a PO number that cannot name a folder of the PO store on every computer: a slash or another sign a folder name cannot hold, more than 100 characters, a device name of Windows; section 6 gives the rule, which holds for a PO PDF as for an export), "is stored for its PO from another file" (a version number that the store holds from another PDF) or "no order line found". The reasons name the setting and never a value of the PDF. It is tried again at every pass, so once the layout is right it is filed by itself. A PDF that was skipped as "other" has moved to `inbox/processed/`: put it into `inbox/` again.

What a layout cannot describe: values that stand under their labels and not beside them, a table without a row of column titles, and a PDF that is a picture (no text layer). Two cells of two characters in all, such as a "1" and a "5" printed close together, stay one word.

**Check a reading before the layout is used.** A layout can read a PO without refusing it and still read it wrong, so compare the reading of a sample PO with the PO itself. The trial read on the settings tab does so (section 3.17): "Testen" shows the header values and the order lines as the layout reads them, with a line for each problem and each warning of the reader, and writes nothing; "Opslaan" refuses a layout that cannot file the sample. Without the page, the reading of a filed PO is in `po_store/<PO>/v<n>/po.json`: the header values with the line each was read from, and each order line with the printed text and the value of every cell. To try a layout that way without touching the real store:

1. Copy `settings.json` into an empty scratch folder. In the copy set `po_store_root` to `"po_store"` and `cases_root` to `"cases"` (they then lie in the scratch folder) and `po_layout` to the layout to try.
2. Put one sample PO into a folder `sample` there, under a name that ends in `_po_v1.pdf`, for example `sample/sample_po_v1.pdf`.
3. Write a file `cases.csv` there that holds only its header row: `case_id,document,po_number,kind`.
4. In the scratch folder run `python -m aid.batch --cases cases.csv --po-dir sample --settings settings.json --reader parser --out summary.csv`. A refused PO stops the command with its reason on the last line; otherwise read `po_store/<PO>/v1/po.json`.

Look first at the PO number. It is the word right after the PO label, on the first line where that word holds a digit, so watch for two things. An earlier line that holds the label too: it is passed by when the word after the label holds no digit (a title "Inkooporder  Pagina 1 van 1", an address "PO Box 61"); when that word holds a digit (a title "Inkooporder 1/2" with the page right after it), the PDF is refused as holding more than one PO number, and a longer label that the title does not hold, such as "Inkooporder:", reads it. And a word between the label and the number: "Inkooporder nr. 26001234" needs the label "Inkooporder nr.", or no PO number is found.

### 3.13 The capture server

The capture server answers the browser extension (section 2.5). It runs beside the runner and the review page, on the computer where the buyer's browser runs. It reads every cart with the cart reader and asks no model, also with `"reader": "llm"`: the pre-check stays fast, needs no Copilot sign-in, and what the rules cannot settle goes to the buyer.

```bash
python3 -m aid.capture_server --settings <root>/settings.json [--host 127.0.0.1] [--port 7870] [--testshop]
```

- `--settings` (required): the settings file, the same one the runner uses, so that the cases land under the same `cases_root`.
- `--host`: the address it listens on, default 127.0.0.1 (this computer only). Leave it so.
- `--port`: default 7870.
- `--testshop`: also serve the made-up cart pages of `aid/madeup/out` under `/testshop/` (for example `http://127.0.0.1:7870/testshop/CART02.html`), for trying it out with the demo's settings. Leave it out on the company side.

It prints the address it listens on and runs until Ctrl-C. How it is kept running is the operator's choice, as for the runner (section 3.4).

**The PO side.** The server looks for a cart's PO only in the PO store, and it files nothing itself. For a cart the PO side is an export of the order lines, because no PO PDF exists before ordering. Save the export from Exact and put it into `inbox/`, or e-mail it so that it reaches the folder of the work mailbox that the runner reads (section 3.14): with `export_columns` set (sections 3.2 and 3.17) the runner files it at its next pass, within a minute, before the PO PDFs and the confirmations of that pass, and moves it to `inbox/processed/`. Each PO of the export becomes a look at that PO, kept beside its PDF versions (section 6, "The export of PO lines"). A cart is checked against its PO as it stands: its latest look when that look was filed after the latest PO PDF, else the PDF. An export whose rows for a PO are those of the PO's latest look files nothing for it, and so does not count as later: the PDF filed after that look keeps standing. An export file is filed once: the same file again, or a file that stays in `inbox/` and is handled again at every pass (one held open in Excel, or an e-mail with another attachment the aid cannot handle yet), files nothing more, also after a newer export. Of several exports that wait for the same pass, the one whose file was saved last (for an attachment, its e-mail) is filed last and stands, whatever their names. Until the cart's PO is in the store, the banner is grey ("Geen inkooporder gevonden") and the case opens as `unknown_<...>`.

**What the export may look like.** An overview of the order lines saved from the ERP as an Excel workbook (.xlsx) or as CSV, with one row per order line. Its columns need not have the aid's names or order: `export_columns` says which column title holds the PO number, the item code and the other values, `export_decimal_comma` how the file writes its numbers, and `export_fill_down` whether the PO number stands only once for each PO (section 3.2); part 2 of the block "Koppeling met Exact (bij de installatie)" on the settings tab sets the three from one sample export, with a trial read (section 3.17). A CSV file may be cut at semicolons, commas or tabs: the aid takes the mark under which it finds the row of column titles. Rows that are no order line are passed over: rows above the column titles, blank rows, the column titles repeated lower down, rows without an item code (totals, group rows, text lines) and, when the map names the quantity column, rows with an item code and no quantity, such as a total that prints its word in the item code column. With `export_fill_down` a line without a PO number belongs to the PO whose number stands nearest above it, also when that number stands on a group row or a text line. Section 6, "The export of PO lines", gives the rules and what makes the aid refuse a file. The runner takes a `.xlsx` or `.csv` file as an export when `export_columns` is set and one of the file's rows holds every column title of the map. While `export_columns` is set, any other `.xlsx` or `.csv` file is skipped as "spreadsheet without the export's column titles" and moved to `inbox/processed/`: a price list, an export with a column title the map does not know or lacks, and a file the aid cannot read as a table (a damaged workbook, a CSV file in an encoding the aid does not read). The digest lists it under "Overgeslagen" with that reason; check the column titles of the map against the file's (section 3.2) and put the file into `inbox/` again. With `export_columns` empty every spreadsheet is skipped as "unsupported file type". A file that the runner takes as an export and the aid then refuses stays in `inbox/`, is listed in the digest under "Overgeslagen" with its reason, which names the row or the column and never a value, and is tried again at every pass: a title of the map that stands twice in the title row, and the refusals of the rows of section 6 (a row with an item code and no PO number without `export_fill_down`, a PO number that is a date, holds no digit or cannot be a folder name, a line number that is blank or stands twice).

**The digest.** The server runs its cases itself and never writes the runner's digest state. The runner, at each pass on the same `cases_root`, reports every case of the capture server that has its result once, so that it appears in the next digest under "voorcontrole". Without a runner on that folder, capture cases are in no digest.

**A case in progress.** While the server runs a case, a new one or one it resumes because an earlier run of it was cut off, the case folder holds a file `.running` (the process id and the start). The runner leaves such a case alone, checking with the time of the check itself, and a start up to ten minutes ahead of its own clock counts as running too (the two processes' clocks may differ); a `.running` file older than ten minutes is a run that crashed, and the runner resumes the case as it resumes any case without a result.

**The token.** Set `capture_token` in `settings.json` to a long random value and put the same value in the extension's options. The extension sends it with every page; a request without it, or with another value, is refused. The value is company-specific and never goes into a repository. The extension keeps it in the browser profile's local extension storage on that computer. The settings tab of the review page shows it, read-only and with a button to copy it, at the end of the part "Koppelingen" as "Code voor de uitbreiding in de browser", on the page of this PC only: a page opened with `--share`, or without its settings file, does not show it. The first start of the folder of section 3.16 writes a random token of 43 characters.

**The extension's options.** The address of the server (default `http://127.0.0.1:7870`; change it only with `--host` or `--port`), the token, and the webshops, one address per line. Saving asks the browser for access to those shops; a shop taken off the list loses that access. An address on `http://127.0.0.1` or `http://localhost` needs no entry: the test shop is always on, and the options page says so. Open tabs of a shop just added need a reload.

**What the process needs.** The server builds its writer and mailer as the runner does: when the settings set the Exact writer (`exact_base_url`), `AID_EXACT_TOKEN` must be in its environment, and when they set SMTP, `AID_MAIL_PASSWORD`, exactly as for the runner. The banner's link is `review_url` with the case, and the review page listens on 127.0.0.1 of its own computer only: start the review page (section 3.7) on the computer where the browser and the capture server run and, when it printed another port, set `review_url` to that address.

**What it logs and keeps.** Each page sent is saved under `cases/captures/` (a page answered by an earlier case is not kept twice, and a page whose pre-check failed before a case was opened is removed). The extension empties in the copy every password field and every field whose name, id or autocomplete holds, in any case and also with its punctuation removed, one of: pass, wachtwoord, card, kaart, cc-, ccnum, cardnumber, creditcard, cvc, cvv, csc, securitycode, iban, rekening. A select so matched has no option chosen in the copy. A field named otherwise keeps its typed value in the copy. Errors go to `cases/capture.log`, one line per capture to the console (section 3.6). A page larger than 5 MB is refused.

**Exit code 2.** The server does not start, and ends with exit code 2 and a line "aid.capture_server: ...", when the settings file cannot be read or is not valid, when `capture_token` is empty, when the writer refuses the Exact settings, when the port is outside 0 to 65535, or when the address cannot be used (the port is taken by another program, for example a capture server that still runs).

### 3.14 Connecting the work mailbox

Instead of a mailbox rule (section 3.3), the aid can read the work mailbox itself. After a sign-in with the buyer's own Microsoft work account on the settings tab of the review page, the runner reads one folder of that mailbox at every pass, through Microsoft Graph, and saves each new e-mail as an `.eml` file into `inbox/`, before it handles the inbox: a confirmation that arrives opens its case in the same pass. The messages that are in the folder when it is connected are left alone; every message that comes into the folder after that is read, also one that an Outlook rule or a drag moves there, whenever it was received. One limit: when the reading was broken for a while (the runner stopped for days, so that Microsoft no longer knows where the aid left off), the aid reads again what was received since its last read, and an older message moved into the folder while the reading was broken is not read; move it out of the folder and in again. The runner only reads the folder: it never moves, flags or deletes a message. The aid's own e-mails that come back into the folder (the digest, the buyer's copy of a question) are passed over. Without a sign-in everything works as before.

**What must reach the folder.** The folder takes the place of the rule of section 3.3, so it must receive the same mail: the e-mails with Exact's PO PDFs, the suppliers' e-mails with their confirmations, and the replies to the aid's questions. A reply arrives in the inbox of the mailbox the question was sent from, so give that mailbox an Outlook rule that moves it into the folder, for example on the subject words "Vraag over orderbevestiging PO" (in English "Question about order confirmation PO"), which a reply keeps after its "RE:". With "Verzenden via" on the SMTP server, the questions go out from the aid's own mailbox (`aid_email`) and the replies arrive there; an Outlook rule cannot move mail into another mailbox, so let that mailbox forward the replies to the work mailbox, or keep the mailbox rule of section 3.3 for it. For the rest, use rules on the sender of Exact's PO e-mails and on the suppliers' sender addresses or domains, or choose "Postvak IN" itself as the folder. Subject words such as "orderbevestiging" or "order confirmation" are examples only: suppliers also write "Opdrachtbevestiging", "Auftragsbestätigung" or "Order acknowledgement", or answer the PO e-mail with the confirmation attached. An e-mail that never reaches the folder is never checked, and nothing reports it; a reply that never reaches it is not filed, and its case waits until its wait ends.

**The app registration.** The sign-in needs an app registration in Microsoft Entra ID. A user can make one in a few minutes when the company allows users to register applications:

1. Open entra.microsoft.com and sign in with the work account.
2. Open App registrations and choose New registration.
3. Name it "Inkoopassistent", choose "Accounts in this organizational directory only" and register it.
4. Under Authentication, add the platform "Mobile and desktop applications" with the redirect URI `http://localhost`, and set "Allow public client flows" to Yes. Save.
5. Under API permissions, add Microsoft Graph, Delegated permissions: `Mail.Read`, `Mail.ReadWrite`, `Mail.Send` and `User.Read`, and, to read and send from another mailbox than your own (below), also `Mail.Read.Shared`, `Mail.ReadWrite.Shared` and `Mail.Send.Shared`. `Mail.ReadWrite` lets the aid create the draft that each of its messages is sent from (Microsoft does not say that a message sent at once keeps the aid's Message-ID, by which a reply is filed); it changes no other message.
6. On the Overview, copy the Directory (tenant) ID and the Application (client) ID into the settings tab. A portal in Dutch calls them "Map-id (tenant)" and "Toepassings-id (client)", as the tab does.

No client secret and no certificate is made: the app is a public client, and the person signs in with their own account. The two IDs are identifiers, not secrets.

**When the portal refuses.** If New registration is greyed out or refused, the account may not register applications: the company decides that, with a setting of Entra ID. Ask IT to make the registration with the settings above, or keep to the mailbox rule of section 3.3. If the sign-in asks for an administrator's approval of the permissions, the company does not let users approve apps themselves: an administrator approves them once.

**Connecting.** Start the review page on the PC where the runner runs (section 3.7), as the same Windows user as the runner (below), and open the tab "Instellingen". The fields and buttons of the connection are at the bottom of that tab, folded away under "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst": a click on that heading opens the part. Everything this section names on the tab is inside it.

1. Fill in the Map-id (tenant) and the Toepassings-id (client).
2. Press "Aanmelden bij Microsoft". A browser window with Microsoft's sign-in page opens on the PC where the page runs: sign in with the work account and accept the permissions. The tab waits for it for at most five minutes. If no window opens, press "Aanmelden met een code": the tab shows an address and a code; open the address on any device, enter the code and sign in, and the tab waits until that is done.
3. The tab shows "Aangemeld als" with the account, and the folders of the mailbox. Only the folders at the top of the mailbox are listed, beside "Postvak IN", not the folders inside them.
4. Leave "Postvak" empty for your own mailbox.
5. Choose the folder and press "Verbinding testen": the tab shows how many messages the folder holds and when the newest arrived, never a subject or a sender.
6. Leave "Verzenden via" on "Automatisch", which sends each message through the mailbox while you are signed in and a folder is chosen, and through the SMTP server or as files when no folder is chosen or no one is signed in (after "Afmelden"); or choose another way. While the sign-in has lapsed, nothing is sent (see "Sending").
7. Press "Opslaan". The six settings are written into the settings file, every other setting kept as it was, and the runner takes them over at its next pass: "Opgeslagen. De assistent gebruikt dit vanaf de volgende ronde, binnen een minuut." Under "Stand van de controle" the tab shows "Nog niet verbonden" until the runner's next pass has connected the folder, then since when the folder is connected, when it was last read and how many messages were saved, or "nog niet gelezen" when that pass could not read the folder (a sign-in to repeat, or a folder the account may not read yet). The line under the sign-in buttons shows who is signed in, read from the PC without asking Microsoft; whether the sign-in still works shows when you press "Verbinding testen" or "Mappen ophalen": "Aanmelding van ... verlopen" if it has lapsed.

**Sending.** With "Automatisch" (once signed in with a folder chosen) or "Het postvak van de aanmelding", the runner's supplier questions, re-approval requests and digest go out from that mailbox, From its address. Each message is first made as a draft in the mailbox and then sent, so that it ends in Sent Items; a draft whose sending failed may stay in the Drafts folder. Who is signed in is looked at for each message, so a sign-in on the tab counts from the next message on, without a restart. A question asked on the review page itself is still written as a file into `cases/outbox/` (section 3.7). While the sign-in has lapsed (the account is still on the PC, but Microsoft no longer accepts it), the supplier questions and re-approval requests are not sent: each case comes to you on the review page with "Bericht niet verzonden", for you to send the message by hand, and no other way is tried, since a reply to another sender would never reach the folder and so never its case. The digest is then written into `cases/outbox/` (section 3.8). Only after "Afmelden" do the messages under "Automatisch" go through SMTP or as files.

**A test with your own mailbox.** Make a folder for the test at the top of your own mailbox (beside "Postvak IN", not inside it), choose it on the tab and save. After the next pass, drag a made-up confirmation e-mail into the folder, or let an Outlook rule move it there. At the next pass (within a minute) it is saved into `inbox/` and its case opens. Use another made-up confirmation for each test: a document the aid has handled once is skipped as "duplicate". To test a reply, answer a question the aid sent to your own address and let the reply reach the folder (see "What must reach the folder").

**Another mailbox.** To read another mailbox, such as the purchasing department's, fill in its address under "Postvak", press "Mappen ophalen", choose its folder and save; the tab refuses a folder listed for another mailbox than the one in the field. Another mailbox or folder starts a new connection from that moment, and the aid sends from that mailbox too. This needs two things: the app registration's shared permissions of step 5, which the aid asks for when a mailbox is filled in (sign in again after filling it in), and the signed-in account's access in Exchange to read that mailbox and to send from it, which IT grants.

**Where the tokens are kept.** The sign-in's tokens are kept on the PC only: on Windows in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Inkoopassistent`, encrypted with Windows' data protection (DPAPI), so that only this Windows user on this PC can read them; elsewhere in `~/.config/inkoopassistent`, encrypted where the system offers a keychain, else in files that only their owner can read. The review page and the runner share them, so both must run as the same Windows user: a runner started as a service account or as SYSTEM looks in another folder, cannot read the page's tokens, and keeps reporting "Postvak niet verbonden" however often the buyer signs in. Copilot (section 3.15) uses the same tokens, one store per Windows user: such a runner cannot ask Copilot either, every case waits with `SignInNeeded`, and the digest says "Copilot werkt niet". The folder remembers the kind of store it was made with, and a process that cannot use that kind is refused: the runner then logs `mailbox: StoreRefused: ...` once in a run and goes on with `inbox/` (section 5). The tokens are never written to the settings file, the repository, a case folder, the audit or a log, and the aid refuses to keep them in a folder inside a git repository.

**Signing out.** "Afmelden" removes the account and its tokens from the PC, and empties the folder list on the tab. Pressed alone, it leaves the folder in the settings file: the runner keeps trying to read the mailbox, handles `inbox/` as before, logs "mailbox: not connected" at most once an hour, and every digest says "Postvak niet verbonden" (section 5); with "Automatisch" its messages go through SMTP or as files. Press "Opslaan" after "Afmelden" to disconnect the mailbox completely: the folder is saved empty, the runner stops reading the mailbox and forgets the connection, and the line goes from the digest. To connect again later, sign in, choose the folder again and save: the connection starts from that moment, as a new one does, and the mail that came into the folder meanwhile is left alone. Saving another folder forgets the connection of the one before in the same way. "Afmelden" ends the sign-in for Copilot too (section 3.15): with "Documenten lezen met" on "Copilot", no case is read and the digest says "Copilot werkt niet" until "Aanmelden voor Copilot" is pressed again or the reader is set to "zonder AI (parser)".

### 3.15 The AI service: Microsoft 365 Copilot

The AI service of the aid is Microsoft 365 Copilot, used with the buyer's own work sign-in of section 3.14 through the Copilot Chat API of Microsoft Graph. With `"reader": "llm"` Copilot reads the confirmations, and it answers the questions the rules leave open: which PO version a document answers, which PO line a line answers, and what kind of document an attachment without a keyword is. The licence covers its use, so the audit record shows no tokens and no cost; it names the conversation of each call by its id. The pre-check in the browser (section 3.13) never asks Copilot: the capture server reads the cart page with the cart reader, whatever `reader` says.

**What Copilot needs.**

- A Microsoft 365 Copilot licence on the account that signs in.
- Seven delegated permissions of Microsoft Graph on the app registration of section 3.14, together: `Sites.Read.All`, `Mail.Read`, `People.Read.All`, `OnlineMeetingTranscript.Read.All`, `Chat.Read`, `ChannelMessage.Read.All` and `ExternalItem.Read.All`. The Chat API asks for all seven, even though the aid sends it only the document's text.
- An administrator's consent for four of them: `People.Read.All`, `OnlineMeetingTranscript.Read.All`, `ChannelMessage.Read.All` and `ExternalItem.Read.All`.

**The app registration.** On entra.microsoft.com, open the app registration of section 3.14:

1. Under API permissions, choose Add a permission, Microsoft Graph, Delegated permissions, and add the seven permissions above (`Mail.Read` is there already).
2. Press "Grant admin consent for" the organisation. Only an administrator can press it: ask IT to press it once, for the four permissions that need it. Without that consent the sign-in for Copilot, or Copilot itself, refuses.

**Connecting.** On the tab "Instellingen" of the review page, open "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst" at the bottom of the tab with a click on its heading. There, under "De AI-dienst: Microsoft 365 Copilot", with the "Map-id (tenant)" and the "Toepassings-id (client)" filled in:

1. Press "Aanmelden voor Copilot". A browser window with Microsoft's sign-in page opens on the PC where the page runs and asks for the permissions of the mailbox and of Copilot together, and for the shared permissions as well when "Postvak" holds another mailbox (section 3.14). Sign in with the work account. The tab says that it waits, for at most five minutes.
2. Press "Copilot testen". The aid sends Copilot one message, "Answer with the word OK only.", and the tab shows "Copilot antwoordt: OK" (the first 20 characters of the answer), or the error, for example "Copilot refused: 403 Forbidden" (section 5).
3. Under "Documenten lezen met", choose "Copilot". The tab writes `"reader": "llm"` into the settings file at once, and the runner takes it over at its next pass. The tab always lists Copilot, but refuses it until the two IDs are saved ("Opslaan"), because the runner refuses `"llm"` without them: it then says so and keeps the reader that is in the file.

**What Copilot is sent.** For a confirmation with a text layer, the aid sends the text of each page, every line starting with its page and line number ("p1 r12: ..."), the instructions of the reading prompt and the schema of the answer. The file itself is not sent and is not put in OneDrive. Copilot reads the text layer only: a scan or a screenshot cannot be read, and its case fails at reading with "Copilot reads the text layer only: this document has no text layer" and is listed under "Mislukt" in the digest, for the buyer to check by hand. A PDF with a scanned page (an image without text) among pages with text is not read in part either: its case fails with "Copilot reads the text layer only: page <n> has no text layer", so that the lines of that page never come out as missing lines. A blank page, with neither text nor an image, is left out. A cart page saved as HTML is read by the cart reader, which needs no AI.

**Grounding and the check.** Web grounding is switched off for every message, so Copilot does not search the web. Enterprise grounding cannot be switched off: Copilot may draw on the user's Microsoft 365 data as its semantic index allows, the mail, chats, meetings, sites and files the account can reach in the organisation. The aid therefore checks every value against the text of the page: a value whose text does not stand on the line it cites (or on the line just before or after it) is marked uncertain and comes to the buyer (R09), so a value Copilot takes from elsewhere is never used as certain.

**A preview of Microsoft.** The Chat API is a preview, in Graph's beta endpoint, which Microsoft does not support for use in production applications. It may change or stop working without notice; then switch back to the parser as below. Microsoft states that using the Chat API is agreeing to its Microsoft 365 Copilot APIs Terms of Use (preview): pressing "Copilot testen" or running the runner with `"reader": "llm"` accepts them, so the company should agree to them first.

**When Copilot fails.** A refusal of Copilot, an outage, a network failure while the sign-in's token is renewed (`Copilot could not be reached: ConnectionError`), or a sign-in that has lapsed, while a case runs, is no failure of the case: the case stays without a result, `runner.log` names the error once under the case id (for example `CopilotError: Copilot refused: 503 no code`, never a token), and a later pass runs it again, after the cases Copilot has not failed, so that a case that keeps failing never holds back the others. A request that Copilot answers with 429, 503 or 504 is sent once more after the wait it asks for, at most 30 seconds.

- **Copilot fails as a whole**: the sign-in has lapsed, the token cannot be had, or the conversation cannot be created, always; or the chat fails while no other case was read since the case's first failure. The other cases of that pass are not sent to Copilot and wait for the next pass too, so that a Copilot that is down or asks the aid to wait is not asked once per case. The digest says "Copilot werkt niet (<the error>): <n> zaken wachten erop" until a pass reads a case through Copilot again. Failures in this state never finish a case.
- **Copilot fails one document**: a conversation that Copilot ends itself ("Copilot ended the conversation: disengagedForRai", its own refusal of that content), a 400 or 413 of the chat that carries the document, or any other failure of that chat after Copilot read another case. The pass goes on with the other cases, and the digest names these documents in a line of their own: "Copilot kon 1 document niet lezen; nieuwe poging bij de volgende ronde". After three refusals of the document, or three failures of its chat in all once Copilot has read another case after the first one, the case is finished as failed with "Copilot could not read this document: <the error>" and listed under "Mislukt", for the buyer to check that document by hand.

An unreadable document (no text layer) is no failure of Copilot: its case is listed as failed, as above.

**Back to the parser.** Choose "zonder AI (parser)" under "Documenten lezen met" (in the same part "Koppelingen" of the settings tab), or set `"reader": "parser"` in the settings file. The runner takes it over at its next pass and reads with the template parser again (section 3.12).

### 3.16 Running from a folder

On a Windows PC where nothing can be installed, the aid runs from a folder that holds its own Python and its packages. The buyer starts it with a double click, and from then on it starts by itself at each logon, without a window. On the PC, every document it handles stays in the folder's `data`; the little the aid puts elsewhere is listed at the end of this section. The folder is made once, on a Windows PC where Python can be used, and copied to the PC where it runs.

**Build.** On a Windows PC with a 64-bit Intel or AMD processor, with the 64-bit Python that will run the aid and its packages installed (section 3.1), from the folder that holds `aid/`:

```
python -m aid.portable --build C:\Users\<you>\Inkoopassistent [--python-zip <file>]
```

- It takes python.org's embeddable package of exactly the Python version that runs the command, downloaded over https from www.python.org, or the file given with `--python-zip` (`python-<version>-embed-amd64.zip`, from the version's release page), which may be the package of another release of the same minor version. The packages are chosen for that version, so build with the Python version the folder is to have.
- python.org has Windows files of 3.11 up to 3.11.9 only (of 3.12 up to 3.12.10, of 3.13 up to 3.13.17): its later releases are source code only. For such a version the download finds no package, and the build says that python.org has no Windows package for this version and builds nothing. Build the folder with a Python of python.org of a version that has one (for 3.11 the last is 3.11.9), or with `--python-zip` and the embeddable package of such a version of the same minor version (for 3.11, `python-3.11.9-embed-amd64.zip`, from the release page of 3.11.9). A file with the name python.org gives it is checked and named by the version in that name.
- The build checks the package's sha256 itself only for the versions whose value the code holds (`EMBED_SHA256` in `aid/portable.py`: 3.13.12 to 3.13.17, 3.14.3 to 3.14.10 and 3.15.0 to 3.15.2, read from their release pages): a package whose sha256 differs is refused, and the build stops and leaves nothing behind. For every other version, 3.11 among them, it checks nothing itself: it prints the package's sha256 and md5 with the address of the version's release page, and the builder compares them with that page before the folder is used (the page of a 3.11 version gives an MD5 only, for "Windows embeddable package (64-bit)").
- It installs the packages of `aid/requirements.txt` with pip, once, with the Python that runs the command, into the folder's `python\Lib\site-packages`, without the library of the development AI client: the folder reads with the parser or with Copilot. Because this Python on this Windows chooses the packages, every package that Windows and this Python version need is installed. pip needs a connection to PyPI and takes a few minutes; when it fails, the build ends with pip's exit code and leaves nothing behind.
- It copies the program into `app\aid`: when the folder that holds `aid/` is a git repository and git can be run, the files git tracks under `aid/`; otherwise the package's `.py` files and the files the code reads (the readers' templates and prompts, the messaging templates, `madeup/out`, `madeup/tryout`, `extension`, `README.md`, `MANUAL.md`, `requirements.txt` and `settings.example.json`). Never the tests, a cache folder or a hidden file. Every other file found under `aid/`, such as the results of an evaluation or a second settings file, is left out, and the build names each in one line: "Left out of app/aid, as no part of the program: ...". Then it writes the three start files and `LEESMIJ.txt`.

```
Inkoopassistent\
  Inkoopassistent starten.cmd                     start the aid and open its page; from then on it starts at each logon
  Inkoopassistent stoppen.cmd                     stop it; it stays off, also at logon, until it is started again
  Inkoopassistent niet meer vanzelf starten.cmd   no longer start it at logon
  LEESMIJ.txt                                     these steps in short, in Dutch, for the buyer
  python\                                         the embeddable Python, with Lib\site-packages
  app\aid\                                        the program
  data\                                           made at the first start, never by the build: settings.json, inbox\, cases\,
                                                  po_store\, logs\ (the <root> of section 3.1)
```

The folder holds the program only: no settings, no case and no purchase order, and no file holds a path of the PC it was built on, so it works wherever it is put. It is about 300 MB in about 11,000 files; the build prints the numbers. A second build into the same folder replaces `python` and `app` and leaves `data` as it is; while the aid runs from the folder, Windows keeps `python` in use, and the build says so and changes nothing. A folder that holds anything but an earlier build, or that lies inside `aid/`, is refused. On another system, or on Windows on another processor, the build builds nothing and says where to run it. Its exit codes: 0 built; 1 the embeddable package could not be had or used (the download, also of a version python.org has no Windows package for, the file, its checksum, its content), the package `aid` could not be copied, or `python` and `app` could not be replaced; 2 refused; pip's own exit code when pip fails.

**Carry.** Copy the whole folder to the PC where the aid runs, onto its own disk, for example `C:\Users\<you>\Inkoopassistent`, and not into a folder that OneDrive synchronises: the documents in `data` stay on the PC. A fresh build has no `data`. To move the folder later, stop the aid, move the folder (move it, never copy it: which look at a purchase order is the newest rests on the times of the files in `po_store`, which a copy can change, section 3.1) and double-click "Inkoopassistent starten" again: the start at logon holds the folder's full paths. The cases and the purchase orders move with `data`: the files written before the move still name the old place, and the aid reads each purchase order's PDF from its own folder in the moved `po_store`, so the review page still shows its pages.

**Start.** Double-click "Inkoopassistent starten". A window opens: "De inkoopassistent start. Even geduld: de pagina opent vanzelf in uw browser." The start file runs `python -I -X utf8 -m aid.portable --start` with the folder's own `python.exe` (every Python the folder starts runs isolated, with `-I`: see the end of this section), which:

1. makes sure that it can write into `data` and `data\logs`. When it cannot, for example in a folder under `C:\Program Files`, on a share that is read-only or on a stick that is write-protected, the window says "De inkoopassistent kan in deze map niet schrijven. Zet de map op een plek waar u mag schrijven, bijvoorbeeld in uw eigen map Documenten, of vraag schrijfrechten op de map data." and nothing is started;
2. writes `data\settings.json` at the first start, and never again: the example settings with the parser as the reader, the folders `cases`, `po_store` and `inbox` beside it, a random capture token of 43 characters, the example's addresses on example.com and the test mode on (`test_mode`, for the owner's test with the test account of Exact, section 3.17; the hand-over switches it off, below); and makes the folders `inbox`, `cases`, `po_store` and `logs`;
3. asks the launcher (`--status`) whether it runs on these settings. When it runs and no `STOP` file stands beside the settings, it only opens the page: "De inkoopassistent draait al. De pagina wordt geopend.";
4. checks, as no launcher runs on these settings, that nothing answers at either of the launcher's two addresses, http://127.0.0.1:7860/ and http://127.0.0.1:7870/health (another aid on this PC, or a review page or a capture server started by hand), and imports the modules the parts need (the self-check: the launcher, the runner, the capture server, the review page with its page library, the made-up set, the price-list import and the image library). Each module that cannot be imported gets a line in `data\logs\start.log` with the class of its error and the first line of its message, so that a package missing from the folder (`ModuleNotFoundError`) can be told from a library that Windows blocks (`ImportError: DLL load failed ...`), and nothing is started;
5. runs the launcher's `--install-autostart` (section 3.4.1): the shortcut `Inkoopassistent.lnk` in the user's Startup folder, which starts the launcher at each logon with the folder's own `pythonw.exe`, without a window, and the launcher started in the background now;
6. when `STOP` still stands beside the settings (after "Inkoopassistent stoppen"), runs `--resume --background`, which waits for a launcher that is still stopping, deletes `STOP` and starts the launcher;
7. asks `--status` again, once a second for at most 90 seconds, whether a launcher now runs: `--install-autostart` ends with exit code 0 once the start at logon is set, also when it started nothing now (section 3.4.1), and on a slow first start, while a virus scanner checks the new files, the launcher can take a minute to come up. While none runs yet, the window says "Even geduld: de inkoopassistent wordt nog opgestart. De eerste keer kan dat een paar minuten duren." When none runs in the end, the start ends there as a start that failed, and `start.log` holds the launcher's reason;
8. waits until http://127.0.0.1:7860/ answers, at most 180 seconds, and opens it in the default browser. The window then closes.

When the start fails, the window says "De inkoopassistent kon niet starten. De reden staat in de map data, in logs, in het bestand start.log." and stays open until a key is pressed. When the page does not answer within 180 seconds (a first start on a slow PC, while Windows checks the new files), it says "De pagina antwoordt nog niet. Open over een minuut zelf http://127.0.0.1:7860 in uw browser."; when no browser can be opened, "De pagina kon niet vanzelf worden geopend. Open zelf http://127.0.0.1:7860 in uw browser." Each launcher command a start file runs gets one line in `data\logs\start.log`, beside the launcher's own lines, `--status` included (of its asks in step 7, the last one): it begins with "folder start:", "folder stop:" or "folder no-autostart:" and gives the command's exit code and what it said. Only one aid runs on a PC (section 3.4.1): when another one runs, for example on the try-out folder of a copy of the repository, stop it first with `--stop` on its own settings, and close a review page or a capture server started by hand in its own window. Its start at logon has the same name, `Inkoopassistent.lnk`, so the folder's start replaces it; `--install-autostart` of the other one brings it back. A proxy that the company sets on the PC never stands between the aid and its own pages, because a proxy cannot reach this PC's own 127.0.0.1 and the review page would end at its start: the review page adds 127.0.0.1 and localhost to `NO_PROXY` before its page library starts, the start and the launcher ask their addresses without any proxy, and every other address, the Microsoft sign-in among them, keeps the PC's proxy.

**Stop.** Double-click "Inkoopassistent stoppen": "De inkoopassistent stopt. Dit duurt hoogstens anderhalve minuut." It runs the launcher's `--stop`, which creates `STOP` beside the settings and waits for the parts to end, at most 90 seconds. When the launcher has ended (its state file `start_state.json` is gone): "De inkoopassistent is gestopt en blijft uit tot u weer op Inkoopassistent starten dubbelklikt." When it is still stopping its parts, for example a runner in the middle of a long pass: "De inkoopassistent stopt nog. Wacht een minuut voordat u de map verplaatst of verwijdert." `STOP` keeps the aid off, also at the next logon, until the start file is used again. The window waits for a key. A stop that fails says "De inkoopassistent kon niet stoppen. De reden staat in de map data, in logs, in het bestand start.log."

**No longer at logon.** Double-click "Inkoopassistent niet meer vanzelf starten": it runs the launcher's `--remove-autostart`, which deletes the shortcut: "De inkoopassistent start niet meer vanzelf wanneer u zich aanmeldt." An aid that runs goes on until it is stopped. When the shortcut cannot be deleted, the window says "Het vanzelf starten kon niet worden uitgezet. De reden staat in de map data, in logs, in het bestand start.log." This window waits for a key too.

**The settings.** `data\settings.json` is the settings file of sections 2.7 and 3.2: the buyer changes their own rules on the settings tab. A value that only the installation sets (the authorization limit, the real addresses, the Exact route) is written into that file while the aid is stopped; the layout of the purchase orders that Exact e-mails is set on the settings tab, in "Koppeling met Exact (bij de installatie)" (section 3.17). The capture token is at the end of the part "Koppelingen" of the settings tab, as "Code voor de uitbreiding in de browser", for the options of the extension, which is loaded unpacked from `app\aid\extension` where the browser allows it (section 2.5).

**Hand-over.** The owner tests the folder with the test account of Exact first (section 3.17, "A test walk with a try-out"); the buyer then gets a folder of their own. The hand-over starts with an empty data folder and the test mode off, in three steps:

1. On the buyer's PC the folder starts with a `data` that holds the settings file of the test and nothing else, without the cases, the purchase orders (`po_store`), `inbox` and the logs of the test, because the test account is a copy of the administration and its order numbers come back with other content. Without a settings file of the test, the first start writes a new one.
2. Switch the test mode off on the settings tab: clear "Proefstand: de assistent werkt met het test-Exact" at the end of the block "Koppeling met Exact (bij de installatie)" (section 3.17), before the buyer works with the aid. The folder's first start writes it on, in a new settings file too, and nothing else ends it: while it is on, the tab "In Exact invoeren" tells the buyer to enter every change in the test Exact only, also once the authorization limit is set. With the aid stopped, `"test_mode": false` in the file does the same.
3. The installation sets the authorization limit in the file (section 3.2), which also keeps the part "Proefdraaien" from offering a try-out and the watched folder from taking one.

**Update.** Build the new version on the PC where the folder is built. On the PC where the aid runs, stop the aid, replace the folders `python` and `app` by those of the new build and start again: `data` stays as it is. A build into the folder of an earlier build does the same on the PC where it is built.

**Remove.** Sign out of Microsoft on the page ("Afmelden", in the part "Koppelingen" of the settings tab), double-click "Inkoopassistent niet meer vanzelf starten", stop the aid and delete the folder.

**What stays on the PC outside the folder.** The shortcut `Inkoopassistent.lnk` (or `Inkoopassistent.cmd`) in the user's Startup folder, until "Inkoopassistent niet meer vanzelf starten". After a Microsoft sign-in on the settings tab, the protected store of the sign-in's tokens in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Inkoopassistent` (section 3.14): "Afmelden" removes the tokens from it, and the folder itself is deleted by hand. In the browser, the history of the page and of the sign-in, and the extension with its options once it is loaded; the page tells the browser to keep none of its files (`Cache-Control: no-store`), so that the page images of a document do not stay in the browser's cache. The aid writes nothing else outside the folder. The review page keeps its temporary files in `data\cases\tmp` (section 3.7). The runner keeps the attachments of an e-mail while it handles the inbox, and the PDF that Copilot reads, in `data\cases\tmp\work` for a moment and removes them again; a part that is ended in that moment can leave such a copy there, inside the folder, until the next start of the runner, which empties that folder. Every Python the folder starts runs isolated (`-I`): the start files, the launcher and its parts, and the start at logon. So it reads none of Python's environment variables and leaves the user's own folder of packages out; the `._pth` file of the folder's Python does not keep these out by itself, because Python reads them before it applies that file. A run of the review page on Linux, with a home folder and a temporary folder of their own, wrote nothing into either while it showed a case; on Windows the live check of the folder is to confirm it.

### 3.17 Connecting to Exact

The aid connects to Exact through what Exact itself delivers, and it changes nothing in Exact. The connection is set on the tab "Instellingen" of the review page, in the block "Koppeling met Exact (bij de installatie)": it is folded away below the suppliers and above "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst", and a click on its heading opens it. Set it at the installation, on the PC where the runner runs. It writes into the settings file that the page was started with, as the rest of the tab does. Part 1 sets the layout of the purchase orders that Exact e-mails, and how the aid finds the purchase order number on a confirmation; part 2 sets the columns of the export of order lines that a cart is checked against before ordering. The block ends with the part "Proefdraaien met een inkooporder uit Exact", which tries the whole chain in the test mode with a made-up confirmation of a purchase order the aid knows (below).

**"Stand van de koppeling".** The top of the block says in seven lines whether the layout of the purchase order is set ("Opmaak van de inkooporder: ingesteld."; until then the aid reads the made-up sample purchase orders only), how many purchase orders the aid knows, from a PO PDF or from an export, and what was filed last with its time: a purchase order with its version, or a purchase order "uit een export" ("Laatst opgenomen: inkooporder 250301 uit een export"), whether the columns of the export are set ("Kolommen van de export: ingesteld." or "Kolommen van de export: nog niet ingesteld."), when the last export was filed ("Laatste export opgenomen: 2026-11-12 09:30.", the time the newest look at a purchase order from an export was filed, or "Nog geen export opgenomen."), how many files in the watched folder the aid could not read: those that lie in `inbox/` longer than two passes of the runner, since every file it handled moves to `inbox/processed/`, how many cases wait for their purchase order ("Zaken die op hun inkooporder wachten: 0."; see "A confirmation that comes before its purchase order" below), and how many changes are still to enter in Exact, counted as the tab "In Exact invoeren" counts them ("Nog in te voeren in Exact: 2."; a "?" for the number while that list cannot be read, section 2.6). The lines name no line of a document, no supplier and no amount, so that they can be passed on in words. "Stand vernieuwen" reads them again, and so does selecting the tab.

**Part 1, "1. Inkooporders die Exact per e-mail stuurt (pdf)".** It writes the settings `po_layout` and `po_senders` (section 3.12) from one sample: a purchase order of the company as Exact e-mails it, a PDF with text.

1. Choose the PDF under "Voorbeeld: een inkooporder uit Exact als pdf". The block shows "De eerste pagina van het voorbeeld, regel voor regel": each line of the first page with its number under "Regel", as the aid reads it. The table shows the first 60 lines of the first page; when the page has more, a line under the table says so: "De tabel toont de eerste 60 regels van de eerste pagina." A scan cannot be used, and the block says so: "Dit pdf-bestand heeft geen tekst (een scan of een afbeelding); de assistent kan het niet lezen." (check A5). Nor can a PDF that is damaged or cut short: "Dit pdf-bestand kan niet worden geopend. Sla het opnieuw op uit de e-mail en kies het nog eens." The field takes any file, and the block refuses a file whose name does not end in .pdf by its own sentence, since the runner, too, takes a PDF by its name: "Dit bestand is geen pdf-bestand: zijn naam eindigt niet op .pdf. Kies de pdf van een inkooporder zoals Exact die verstuurt."
2. Copy the texts that stand before the values from that table: "Tekst voor het inkoopordernummer" (required), "Tekst voor de datum (mag leeg)", "Tekst voor de leverancier" or "Of: het nummer van de regel waarop alleen de leverancier staat" (a number of the column "Regel"; one of the two, or neither), the text before the version number (empty when the PDF names no version: the versions of a purchase order are then numbered in the order they arrive), "Tekst voor de valuta (mag leeg)" and "Tekst voor de leveringsconditie (mag leeg)". A text may be several words, such as "Inkooporder nr.": the aid looks for them together, as a whole. Leave a field empty for a value the PDF does not print.
3. Type one word of the row of column titles under "Een woord uit de kopregel boven de orderregels, bijvoorbeeld Artikel" and press "Kopregel zoeken". The block takes the first line of the first page that holds that word as a word of its own and shows a dropdown for each word of that line, labelled with the word. Choose for each word what its column holds: "Regelnummer", "Artikelcode", "Omschrijving", "Aantal", "Eenheid", "Prijs", "Prijs per (aantal)", "Korting in procenten", "Nettoprijs" or "Leverdatum"; "Hoort bij de kolom links ervan" for the second word of a title of two words, such as "levering" in "Gewenste levering"; "Niet nodig" for a column the aid does not read, such as a line amount. One column must be "Artikelcode", and one "Regelnummer" or "Aantal". The block takes a row of at most 20 words.
4. Under "De tabel houdt op bij de regel met deze tekst (leeg: tot het eind)", copy the whole text of the row right under the last order line, for example the total row; a subtotal per page with the same text ends the table on that page. Choose how the PDF writes its numbers under "Getallen op de pdf", and tick "De pdf heeft geen kolom prijs per: elke prijs is per 1 stuk" when the PDF has no column for what a price is per (check A6, question i).
5. Under "Afzender van de e-mails met inkooporders uit Exact", fill in the address that Exact sends the purchase orders from (check A4), or only the part after the at sign; several with a comma between them. Fill it in: a PDF in this layout from another sender is then handled as an order confirmation, so that a supplier's confirmation that looks like the purchase order is never filed as one.
6. Press "Testen". It writes nothing. Under "Zo leest de assistent het voorbeeld" the block shows two tables: the header values that the layout reads, and a row for each order line with its line number and the columns of the layout. A value stands as the aid will file it, with the text the PDF prints in brackets when the two differ: a date as year-month-day, a unit as the aid names it ("pc" for "st"), and a number in plain digits, with a point for the decimals and no zeros at the end. So twelve and a half printed as "12,50" stands as "12.5 (12,50)", and the printed "1.000" stands as "1000 (1.000)" with "1.234,50 (komma voor de decimalen)" but as "1 (1.000)" with "1,234.50 (punt voor de decimalen)": look at every number, since the wrong choice under "Getallen op de pdf" can turn a thousand into one. "?" is a value that could not be read, with the printed text in brackets when there is one. Below the tables stands a line for each problem and each warning of the reader (below). Only when the reading has no problem, a line above the tables says how many order lines the block read and whether the total of the purchase order can be worked out, and ends "Klopt alles, druk dan op Opslaan". The total itself, the authorization limit and whether the total is above it are never shown.
7. Compare the tables with the purchase order in Exact, value by value. When all is right, press "Opslaan".

**What the reading names.** A problem keeps the sample from being filed with the layout, and "Opslaan" is refused with it ("Niet opgeslagen: ..."): each problem is named in the page's language and nothing is written. The problems are: no sample chosen; a form that gives no layout (an empty text before the PO number, both the supplier's text and its line, no title row found, a word without a meaning, a first word that is to belong to the column on its left, one meaning for two columns, no "Artikelcode", neither "Regelnummer" nor "Aantal"); a PDF without text, or one that cannot be opened; a first page without the title row ("De kopregel van het voorbeeld is anders dan de kopregel die u hebt ingevuld."); no PO number after its text; different numbers after the text before the PO number, named by their lines in the table of the first page (or by their pages, for a later page), since the aid would take the PDF for more than one purchase order and file none of it: when only one of those lines holds the PO number, copy a longer text before the number that stands only on that line, such as "Inkooporder:" (section 3.12, "Check a reading before the layout is used"); a PO number that cannot be the name of a folder; a line above the title row that holds a word of an order confirmation, since the aid would take such a PDF for a confirmation (section 3.12, "Which PDF is a PO"); a column of numbers printed with one, two, four or more digits after a comma while "1,234.50 (punt voor de decimalen)" is chosen, or after a point while "1.234,50 (komma voor de decimalen)" is chosen, named by its column with the choice that fits (a mark followed by exactly three digits, as in "1.000", tells neither); no order line; an order line without an item code or without a quantity; and a sender that is neither an address nor a domain. A warning refuses nothing; it says what the reader passes by: a line of the first page on which the text before the PO number is followed by no word with a digit, and a page that does not repeat the title row, which is read with the columns of the title row before it, or not at all once the end text has ended the table. A line of the table of the first page is named "Regel" with its number there and "van de eerste pagina" ("Regel 4 van de eerste pagina"); an order line is named "Regel" with its own line number. Problems and warnings name the setting, the column, the line or the page, never a value of the PDF.

**What a save changes.** "Opslaan" writes `po_layout` and `po_senders` and nothing else: every other key of the settings file keeps its place and its bytes (section 3.2), and saving the same values again writes nothing. The block says "Opgeslagen. De assistent leest inkooporders in deze opmaak vanaf de volgende ronde, binnen een minuut. Inkooporders die nog in de bewaakte map liggen omdat de assistent ze niet kon lezen, worden dan opgenomen; andere geeft u opnieuw." The runner takes the layout over at its next pass. From then on every PO PDF is read with this layout and with nothing else, and the PO PDFs that stayed in `inbox/` because the aid could not read them are filed at that pass; a PDF that was skipped as "other" has moved to `inbox/processed/`: put it into `inbox/` again. While a layout or a sender is set, the review page's `--share` is refused. The block is filled with the saved layout and senders each time the page is opened and each time the tab "Instellingen" is selected, so that another sample can be tested with it without searching the title row again. A saved title row of more than 20 words, which the form cannot show, leaves the title row and its dropdowns empty, with the line "De opgeslagen kopregel heeft meer dan 20 woorden; dat kan dit formulier niet tonen. De opmaak blijft gelden tot u een nieuwe opslaat.": the saved layout stays in force. When the layout or the senders were changed after the block was filled, in another browser tab or in the settings file itself, "Opslaan" writes nothing and says "Niet opgeslagen: de koppeling is intussen op een andere plek gewijzigd. Open het tabblad Instellingen opnieuw en kijk de opmaak na." Then go to another tab and back to "Instellingen", so that the block is filled with what the file now holds, and check the layout before saving again.

**The purchase order number on a confirmation.** The aid links a confirmation to its purchase order by the number that stands after a word such as "Uw inkooporder" or "Your PO", in the e-mail or on the first page: the setting `po_number_pattern` (section 3.2). Two fields at the end of part 1, under their own heading "Hoe de assistent het inkoopordernummer op een orderbevestiging vindt", set it: "Woorden waar leveranciers uw inkoopordernummer achter zetten", with a comma between the words ("Met een komma ertussen, bijvoorbeeld: Inkooporder, Uw referentie, Uw ordernummer."), and "Een inkoopordernummer heeft minstens zoveel cijfers" "en hoogstens zoveel cijfers". Fill in the words that the suppliers print before your order number on the confirmations kept with the orders in the test Exact (check A10) and the number of digits of your order numbers (check A9), and press "Nummerherkenning opslaan". It writes `po_number_pattern` and nothing else: the words, without regard to case, then spaces, a colon, a hash, a full stop or a dash, then a number of that many digits. A space inside a word stands for any run of spaces or a line break. The fields are filled with the pattern of the settings file each time the page is opened and the tab "Instellingen" is selected; out of the box they show the five words "PO, Inkooporder, Bestelling, Bestellung, Order" and six to eight digits. A number that more digits follow is never cut short: a longer number is taken whole or not at all, in the default as in the fields' form; the two differ only in that the fields' form also passes a full stop between the word and the number. The page then says "Opgeslagen. De assistent gebruikt dit vanaf de volgende ronde, binnen een minuut."; fields left as they were filled save nothing ("Er is niets gewijzigd; er is niets opgeslagen."), so the default stays in force until a word or a number of digits is changed. A save without a word, or with a number of digits that is no whole number from 1 to 20 or a smallest above the largest, is refused and writes nothing, naming the field with its problem: "vul minstens een woord in", "een heel getal van 1 tot 20, en het kleinste niet groter dan het grootste". A pattern of another form, written into the settings file by hand, is shown as "eigen patroon (in het instellingenbestand)" in the empty field of the words and is left alone while the fields stay empty; filled in, the fields replace it. When the pattern was changed after the fields were filled, in another browser tab or in the file, the save writes nothing and says "Niet opgeslagen: de nummerherkenning is intussen op een andere plek gewijzigd. Open het tabblad Instellingen opnieuw en kijk de woorden en de cijfers na." Then go to another tab and back to "Instellingen", so that the fields are filled with what the file now holds, and check the words and the digits before saving again. A changed number recognition counts for documents that come in after the change; on a confirmation that came in before it, you decide.

The pattern alone does not decide. When it finds two or more different numbers, the one counts that the PO store holds with one of that order's item codes on the first page as a word of its own; when none of them counts, or more than one, the document is skipped as "more than one PO number" and listed in the digest, as before. When it finds none, a number the store holds counts when it stands in the e-mail or on the first page as a number of its own, at least four characters long, and one of that order's item codes stands on the first page as well. A number of its own is not joined to letters or digits by a dash, a full stop, a slash or a comma: so the year of a date ("21-10-2026"), the digits of an article code ("KM-1001") and those of an amount ("1250,00") never count, while a number at the end of a sentence or before a comma and a space does ("Uw referentie 250101.", "250101, 250102"), and so does a number after a label and a colon ("Uw referentie:250101"). So a confirmation that names the order under a word the pattern does not know is still linked, also to an order number with letters in it, while a number that happens to be on the page links nothing without an item code of its order. Exactly one such number gives the case its order; otherwise the aid looks, as before, for the one order that holds at least two of the confirmation's item codes. A confirmation linked in any of these ways, by a held number among several numbers, by a held number without the pattern, or by its lines, is the aid's guess: it comes to you with the reason "Kijk na of dit de goede inkooporder is: de assistent vond haar niet aan het nummer achter een van de woorden van de nummerherkenning.", and nothing is written or asked for it, as for a new supplier (section 2.4). When the aid does not know the version such a confirmation quotes, its case has "Geen versie van de inkooporder gekozen" beside that reason, as nothing was compared, and its order pane ("Inkooporder") is empty because no version was chosen. It has no hint and is not counted, as a guess makes no promise, but until you decide on it the runner checks it again once that version is filed, as below. When a supplier's confirmations keep coming with this reason, add the word that supplier prints before your order number to the number recognition, so that its next confirmations are linked by their own number.

**A confirmation that comes before its purchase order.** A confirmation that names an order the aid does not know yet, or an order the aid knows without the version the confirmation quotes, waits for it: its case is in review with "Geen versie van de inkooporder gekozen", and the tab "Beoordelen" says above the documents which order or which version the aid does not know yet, for example "Inkooporder 250101 kent de assistent nog niet. ..." (section 2.2). The line "Zaken die op hun inkooporder wachten" under "Stand van de koppeling" counts these cases. At each pass, after it has handled `inbox/`, the runner checks such a case again once its order, or the version the confirmation quotes, is filed and the store holds it: for the case's own order number, never for another. An export that files a new or changed look at an order counts as filing that order, and at the first pass after the runner starts every order of the store counts as filed. A confirmation on which the aid found no order number waits for nothing: it has no hint, is not counted, and stays in review for you. Still, when the runner files an order in a pass, or at its first pass after a start, it links such a case to that order when the scan would link the same document to it now: by the one order number the store holds that stands on its first page with one of that order's item codes, or else by its lines. That link is a guess as well, so the new case comes to you with its reason (above). It is not made once the number recognition finds a number on the confirmation's first page, because the recognition was changed after the confirmation came in: such a case stays with you. Checked again means: a new case is opened from the same document with the order's number and runs in the same pass, as any case does; the old case is closed as replaced ("PO 250101: afgesloten, geen schrijfacties", without a supplier, with the record `{"action": "replaced", "by": <the new case id>}` in its `steps/09_close.json` and its audit record). It leaves the review page and the digest's list of cases, a decision recorded on it afterwards is never carried out (the close refuses a case that is not in review), and it does not count toward its supplier's first cases. When the runner stopped after it opened the new case and before it closed the old one, the next check closes the old case as replaced by that case and opens no other. A case on which you decided or which you closed, or which wrote or sent anything, is never checked again: decide on it as on any other case. A decision you save while the runner checks the case again stops that check, unless you save it in the very moment the runner closes the old case as replaced; the close then says so: "Deze zaak kan niet worden afgesloten: zij is niet meer ter beoordeling." When the check stops, the new case it opened for the confirmation is removed, and so is a new case that an earlier check opened when it has not run yet (it has no result and wrote or sent nothing); a new case that has run stays.

**Where the sample is.** The sample stays on this PC. When it is chosen, the page library receives it in its own temporary folder, `cases/tmp` under `cases_root` (section 3.7), or the folder that the environment variable `GRADIO_TEMP_DIR` names when it names one. The aid reads it there, copies it nowhere and files nothing from it. The page removes the file from that folder between one and two hours after it was chosen, and at its next start; to have it gone at once, delete it there once the layout is saved.

**Set the layout up alone, not on a shared screen.** The table of the first page and the reading show a real purchase order of the company, with its supplier and its prices. Set the layout up at the PC itself, not on a shared screen or in a recorded meeting.

**The part "Proefdraaien met een inkooporder uit Exact".** The last part of the block. Without a supplier and without the AI service, the owner can still try the whole chain on a purchase order of the test account of Exact: the aid makes a made-up order confirmation of a purchase order it knows, with one difference the owner chooses, and handles it as it handles any confirmation.

- The switch "Proefstand: de assistent werkt met het test-Exact" sets the test mode (`test_mode`, section 3.2) as soon as it is ticked or cleared: it writes that setting and nothing else, and says "Opgeslagen.". When it turns the test mode off while the list "In Exact invoeren" still holds changes that only try-out documents asked, it says how many: "Opgeslagen. Op de lijst In Exact invoeren staan nog 2 wijzigingen uit proefdocumenten. Voer die niet in het echte Exact in; laat ze vallen." When the test mode in the file changed after the block was filled (in another browser tab, or in the file itself), it writes nothing, shows the switch as the file now holds it and says "De proefstand is intussen op een andere plek gewijzigd; de schakelaar toont nu wat het instellingenbestand bevat." The switch then shows the test mode as it stands: toggle it only if that is not what you want.
- The part offers a try-out only in the test mode and while no authorization limit is set (`authorization_limit` is `null`). Otherwise it shows one line: "Proefdraaien kan alleen in de proefstand en zolang er geen fiatteringsgrens is ingesteld." The watched folder takes a try-out under the same two conditions only: one made earlier and given to it after the limit was set, or after the test mode was switched off, by mail or by hand, is skipped as "try-out document outside the test mode", which the digest names, and moves to `inbox/processed/`, so that no request to approve an order again and no question to a supplier can come of it.
- "Inkooporder" lists the purchase orders the aid knows, by their number and supplier, the one filed last first and chosen. The list is filled when the page opens, each time the tab "Instellingen" is selected and with "Stand vernieuwen" at the top of the block, which keeps the order chosen while the aid knows it; a button pressed while no order is chosen fills it first and takes the order filed last. While the aid knows none, the part says "De assistent kent nog geen inkooporders. Laat eerst een inkooporder uit Exact binnenkomen.", also after a switch of the page's language.
- "Wat wijkt af?" offers "Niets: alles komt overeen", "De prijs van de eerste regel is 1 procent hoger", "De prijs van de eerste regel is 10 procent hoger", "De leverdatum van de eerste regel is drie dagen later" and "De leverdatum van de eerste regel is zestig dagen later". The change goes on the first line that states a price, or a delivery date. A new price is rounded half up to the decimals the order prints, at least two, and is at least one step of them higher (12,50 becomes 12,63; 12 becomes 12,12 and 0,125 becomes 0,126), so that the change is the 1 or 10 percent its label says. No choice changes a quantity or leaves a line out: about those the aid would ask the supplier, and nothing made up may reach a supplier, the approver or Exact.
- "Proefbevestiging maken (pdf)" makes the PDF, "PROEF orderbevestiging <number> <time>.pdf", and offers it for download: "De proefbevestiging is gemaakt. Stuur haar als bijlage naar uw eigen postvak, naar de map die de assistent leest, of geef haar direct aan de assistent." The PDF lies in the page's temporary folder (section 3.7) and goes as the page's other temporary files go. The download shown goes when you choose another order or another change, and when you give the try-out directly, so that the file offered is always the one of the choice shown.
- "Proefbevestiging direct aan de assistent geven" puts it into `inbox/`, under a name that starts with a dot until it is whole, so that the runner never reads half a file: "De proefbevestiging ligt in de bewaakte map. De assistent opent de zaak bij de volgende ronde, binnen een minuut." A copy cut off in the middle (the page ended, the PC switched off) keeps that name, which the runner never reads, and the runner removes it at its next start once it is older than ten minutes. From the watched folder on the try-out is an ordinary case: scanned, run and listed like any other, but that the aid sends nothing about it (below); the page writes nothing into the purchase orders or the cases.

The try-out says in its first row "Proefleverancier (verzonnen document)" and under its table "Dit is een proefdocument van de inkoopassistent. Het komt niet van een leverancier." It is drawn in the made-up layout that the aid reads without AI (section 3.12), with every line of the purchase order as the aid holds it, each value only when the order states it: the item code, its column as wide as the longest item code of the order needs, at the cost of the description, so that an item code of up to about 100 points at the document's font size prints whole, such as twenty digits or "KM-08-00417-ABC-2026", while a wider one, such as twenty capitals, is refused with "een artikelcode is te lang"; the description, of a line matched by its item code cut after its last word that fits its column, with " …" after it, and of a line without an item code, or with one without a letter or a digit such as "-", printed whole, since the aid matches such a line by its description; the quantity, the unit, the price (the net price when the order states a discount or a net price), the price basis and the delivery date; and the currency and the delivery terms when the order states them. It quotes the version it was made from ("Uw inkooporder 250108, versie 1"), so that the aid compares it with that version; a purchase order known from an export only has no version to quote, and its try-out is compared with what the export showed. Each try-out is a document of its own: the moment it was made, to the second, is in the PDF's title, which the aid does not read, so that a second try-out of the same order and change, given directly and then mailed, opens a case of its own and is not skipped as a duplicate.

**The read-back.** Before a try-out is offered for download or given to the assistant, the aid reads it back as the runner reads it without AI and compares it with the version of the order it was made from. It makes the try-out only when the only difference is the change chosen (with "Niets: alles komt overeen", none at all), only when the assistant would find its order, and only when it would know the version of the order to compare it with. It finds the order by its number, which the aid reads for an order number of six to eight digits (the PO number pattern of the settings, section 3.2), or else by a held number, the number of an order the aid knows, of at least four characters, that stands on the try-out with one of that order's item codes ("The purchase order number on a confirmation", above), or else by its lines, which find an order only when exactly one order the aid knows has two of its item codes. A try-out found by a held number or by its lines is the aid's guess, as any confirmation linked so: its case comes to you with the reason "Kijk na of dit de goede inkooporder is: de assistent vond haar niet aan het nummer achter een van de woorden van de nummerherkenning." and writes nothing before you decide (above). So a try-out is made only when the assistant would find its order; a try-out the read-back cannot place is refused with its reason, such as one of an order with one line whose number has fewer than four characters and is not one the pattern reads, or of an order of such a number whose lines a repeat order shares. A purchase order that gives no try-out is refused, nothing is written, and the part says why: "Van deze inkooporder kan geen proefdocument worden gemaakt: zij heeft geen orderregels." or, after the same beginning, "de eerste regels hebben geen prijs", "de eerste regels hebben geen leverdatum", "een tekst bevat een teken dat het proefdocument niet kan afdrukken" (a character that the PDF's built-in font lacks, anywhere in what the try-out prints, such as the ń of "FCA Poznań" in the delivery terms or a Greek μ in a unit: it would print as another letter, and the aid would ask the supplier about it), "een artikelcode is te lang" (an item code wider than about 100 points, such as twenty capitals, which would leave the description less room than its title), "een waarde past niet in het proefdocument" (a unit, a price basis, a quantity, a price or a delivery date too wide for its column, such as the unit "verpakkingseenheid" or the price basis 10000; the whole description of a line without an item code that does not fit its column; a line with neither), "het proefdocument zou niet precies zo worden gelezen als de inkooporder" (any other difference the read-back finds), "de assistent zou de inkooporder van het proefdocument niet vinden" and "de assistent zou niet weten met welke versie van de inkooporder zij moet vergelijken" (an order the aid finds, but whose version it could not choose, such as an order of another number with two versions of the same lines).

**A case made from a try-out sends nothing.** The aid knows such a case by the first row of its document. No message about it is sent or written, whatever its rules or a misreading make of it: a question to the supplier, or a request to the approver, is recorded in the case as not sent, "not sent (try-out document: nothing is sent)", and each of its items comes to you on the tab "Beoordelen" with the reason "Dit is een proefdocument: de assistent stuurt hierover niets naar een leverancier."; "Leverancier vragen" on such a case is refused with the same sentence. On the tab "In Exact invoeren" each change that only try-outs asked carries "(proefdocument)" behind its row, in the test mode and out of it (section 2.6).

**The order at hand-over: the test mode off, the layout first, the mailbox second.** At hand-over the steps left are switching the test mode off, connecting to Exact and connecting the buyer's mailbox, all on this tab. ## 4. Maintaining it

### 4.1 Adding a supplier

1. On the settings tab of the review page, under "Leveranciers", choose "Leverancier toevoegen", type the supplier's name as the PO prints it, its address for the questions and its lead time in days, and press "Leverancier opslaan" (section 2.7). This writes `supplier_emails` and `supplier_lead_times`; editing those two in `settings.json` is the other way. A supplier that is listed already is not added a second time: choose it under "Leverancier" and change its values there. A new supplier needs an address, a lead time or a tick besides its name.
2. If the supplier's confirmations have a layout no template reads, add a parser template.

A template is a JSON file `aid/readers/templates/<name>.json`; the file name is the template's `name`. Files whose name starts with `po_` are PO templates and are not used for confirmations. Templates are tried in the order of their file names; the first whose `detect` expressions all match the document's text is used. Look at `table_nl.json` (a table) and `text_en.json` (one sentence per line) as examples. The fields:

- `name`, `language` (`"nl"`, `"de"` or `"en"`), `decimal_comma` (true for "1.234,50");
- `detect`: regular expressions that must all match somewhere in the text;
- `header`: per field (`po_reference`, `version_hint`, `supplier`, `document_date`, `currency`, `incoterm`) an expression with exactly one group, the value;
- `table` with `"strategy": "lines"`: `header_row_contains` (text on the title row), `columns` (each a `title` word and the `field` it fills: `line_id`, `item_code`, `description`, `quantity`, `unit`, `unit_price`, `price_basis`, `discount_pct`, `net_price`, `delivery_date`; `line_id` is required), and optionally `line_id_pattern`, `basis_mark` and `item_code_regex`;
- or `table` with `"strategy": "text"`: `line_regex` with named groups after those fields (`line_id` required), and optionally `line_start` and `item_code_regex`.

A misspelled key or a bad expression is refused when the template loads. Test a template on one document:

```bash
python3 -B -c "
from pathlib import Path
from aid.readers.supplier_parser import load_templates, detect_template, read_with_template
pdf = Path('aid/madeup/out/MU01_conf.pdf')
t = detect_template(pdf, load_templates())
print(t.name if t else 'no template fits')
doc = read_with_template(pdf, t)
for line in doc.lines: print(line.line_id, line.item_code.value, line.quantity.value, line.unit_price.value, line.delivery_date.value)
"
```

Or run the batch (section 6) with a one-line `cases.csv` and look at `steps/02_read_document.json` of the case. Add a test beside those of `aid/tests/test_supplier_parser.py` and run the suite. A template for a real supplier is company material: it stays on the company side and never goes into a repository; only the made-up templates are in the repository.

### 4.2 Changing thresholds

Change them on the settings tab of the review page and press "Opslaan" (section 2.7); editing `settings.json` is the other way, and the tab shows such a change the next time it is selected. The runner reads the file again at the start of its next pass when the file has changed (a file that is not valid is logged in `runner.log` and the settings in use are kept); the capture server reads it before its next pre-check and the review page before its next close or undo, so neither needs a restart. A case that was handled before the change keeps its outcome, and on the same day a cart that was checked before is checked anew only when the cart itself changes (section 2.7). The thresholds are `price_pct`, `price_abs`, `lead_time_tolerance_days`, `ask_wait_working_days` and `first_cases_review`; the supplier lists are `review_suppliers` and `messages_off_suppliers`. Each case's audit record keeps the settings it ran with. During the evaluation the settings must not change (section 4.8).

### 4.3 The price-list import and its undo

```bash
python3 -m aid.pricelist --file <list.csv or list.xlsx> --supplier <supplier> --current <current_prices.csv> --prepared <folder> (--write-log <folder> | --settings <root>/settings.json)
```

- `--file`: the price list, CSV (comma or semicolon) or the first sheet of an `.xlsx`, with the columns `item_code` and `price`, and optionally `basis`, `unit`, `min_quantity`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`.
- `--supplier`: the supplier, recorded with every change.
- `--current`: the current prices, a CSV file with the columns `item_code` and `price`.
- `--prepared`: the folder for `prepared_changes.csv`; give `<root>/cases/prepared`, so that the changes stand on the tab "In Exact invoeren" (section 2.6).
- `--write-log`: the folder whose `write_log.jsonl` gets every change; without it, `--settings` gives `<cases_root>/pricelists/<YYYY-MM-DD>`. One of the two is required.

The import always prepares its changes; it writes nothing to Exact. It prints a line per row (updated, skipped with the reason, or error) and a summary, and warns when rows are past `valid_to`. Exit code 0 when it ran, 1 when a file cannot be read or a write failed, 2 for a wrong command line. To undo an import, reverse its write-log folder with `undo_case` (as in section 3.10, with that folder in place of the case folder and a `PreparedWriter` or the runner's writer). Prepared into `<root>/cases/prepared`, the undo takes the import's article prices that were not ticked as entered off the list, and asks to put the old price back for those that were (section 3.10). One article price of a list that is in Exact is put back from the tab with "Terugdraaien in Exact" (section 2.6), when the import kept its write log in a folder under `<cases_root>/pricelists/` (the default with `--settings`).

### 4.4 Re-running a case

A case is run once. The runner never runs a case again that holds `steps/08_result.json`, because a second run would make its changes again and send its questions again. A case whose run was cut off before any change or message is simply run at the next pass. A case cut off after a change or a message is not run again: it goes to review with the item "run cut off after writes: check the write log". Then the researcher compares its `write_log.jsonl` (and `messages/sent.jsonl`) with Exact and the mailbox, finishes by hand what is missing, and the buyer decides on the page.

A confirmation that waits for its purchase order is checked again by the runner itself, never run again in its own folder: when that order, or the version the confirmation quotes, is filed, a new case is opened from the same document and runs, and the first case is closed as replaced, with the new case's id in its `steps/09_close.json` and its audit record (section 3.17). At the first pass after the runner starts every order of the store counts as filed, so a waiting case is checked again then only when its order, or the version it quotes, is in the store. Both folders stay, the first one for the audit (section 3.1). This happens only while nothing was done on the first case: a case on which the buyer decided, which was closed, or which wrote or sent anything is never checked again. A changed number recognition counts for documents that come in after the change; on a case that came in before it, the buyer decides.

To run a case again on purpose (after a fix, and only when its changes were undone or never made), through Python:

```python
import os
from pathlib import Path
from aid.settings import load_settings
from aid.runner import build_deps
from aid.case import load_case
from aid.pipeline import run_case, record_dispositions

settings = load_settings(Path("<root>/settings.json"))
deps = build_deps(settings, os.environ)
case = load_case(settings.cases_root, "<case id>")
result = run_case(case, deps, rerun=True)
record_dispositions(case, result)
```

This makes the changes and sends the questions again, and adds rows to `dispositions.csv`. A safer way to try a document again is the batch (section 6): it opens a new case folder, prepares its changes and sends nothing. Sending the same file to the inbox again does not work: a document seen before is skipped as "duplicate". For a confirmation that waits for its order, give the order instead (section 3.3).

### 4.5 Cleaning old cases

During the evaluation, delete nothing under `cases/` and `po_store/`. The evaluation needs each case folder whole (`case.json`, `input/`, `steps/`, `audit.json`, `write_log.jsonl`, `dispositions.csv`, `decisions.jsonl`, `messages/`), and `cases/prepared/` (`prepared_changes.csv` and the buyer's ticks in `marks.jsonl`). Deleting a case folder also loses its undo, makes a re-sent copy of its document open a new case, and lowers the count of closed cases that rule R11 uses for its supplier.

What may go after it has been read: the files in `inbox/processed/` (each case keeps its own copy in `input/`, and each PO its copy in `po_store/`), old files in `cases/digests/`, the `.eml` files in `cases/outbox/` once handled, and an old `runner.log` while the runner is stopped. After the evaluation, archive the case folders to the company's restricted storage rather than deleting them.

### 4.6 Backups

Back up the whole `<root>` folder, at a moment when the runner is stopped or between passes. It holds company data (documents, addresses, the authorization limit in `settings.json`), so the backup stays on the company side, in storage the company names. The credentials are not in the folder; they stay in the secret store.

### 4.7 Updating the code

1. Create `STOP` and wait for the runner to end; stop the review page.
2. In the copy of the thesis repository: `git pull`.
3. Install the packages again and run the suite:

```bash
python3 -m pip install -r aid/requirements.txt -r aid/requirements-dev.txt
python3 -m pytest aid/tests -q
```

4. Only when every test passes: delete `STOP` and start the runner and the review page again.

Development happens in the thesis repository. The aid's own repository, Luuuuuig/Checking_aid_BEP, is a copy of `aid/` and of the two templates its tests read (`evaluation/templates/dispositions.csv` and `references.csv`). A sync copies these again by hand: copy them, run the suite in the copy, commit and push there. Nothing does this automatically.

### 4.8 The freeze

```bash
python3 -m aid.freeze --settings <root>/settings.json [--out <file>]
```

It writes the freeze record (default `freeze_<date>.json` in the current folder): the git commit and whether the working tree is clean, the sha256 of the settings file and the settings themselves (the authorization limit only as "set" or "unset"), the prompt files with their versions and hashes, the model, the rules R01 to R11 with the settings they read and their values, the aid version, the Python version and the installed packages. The record holds the settings' addresses and supplier names, so it stays on the company side.

During the evaluation nothing of this may change: no `git pull`, no change to `settings.json` (its hash is recorded), no new template or prompt, no package update. Freeze from a clean working tree. If something must change, record a new freeze and note the deviation as the evaluation protocol says.

### 4.9 Known limitations of the pre-check in the browser

- **A cart changed after a small price difference asks for the same change again until a new export comes.** When the aid accepted a small price difference on a cart (R04) and the buyer then changes the cart, the new case is checked against the same look at the order, the one the last export gave, and prepares the same change, or makes the same write, again. A look holds what the export showed when it was filed and does not learn the aid's own changes. Once the change is entered in Exact, a new export of the order lines is the next look at the order, and a cart checked after it is compared with the order as it then stands.
- **Closed cart cases count for the first cases of a supplier.** A cart case that closes counts toward `first_cases_review` of its supplier, like a closed confirmation, so a few pre-checks can end the first-cases review of that supplier.
- **The capture server and the runner share the cases.** Both may use the same `cases_root` on one computer. The runner never runs a case again that has a result, and it leaves a case alone while the server's `.running` file in it is younger than ten minutes. A run of the server that takes longer than that (a slow Exact route, for example) can therefore be run a second time by the runner, and a case whose run crashed waits up to ten minutes before the runner finishes it.

## 5. Troubleshooting

**A case failed.** It is in the digest under "Mislukt" with the first line of its error, and not on the review page. Look in the case folder: `steps/08_result.json` (status `failed`, field `error`, which names the change it failed at, if any), `audit.json` (`errors`, with the step and, for an unexpected error, the traceback), and the last step file in `steps/`, which shows how far it got. A confirmation whose layout no template fits fails at reading. Fix the cause, then use the batch to try the document again (section 4.4).

**A document was not recognised.** See the "Overgeslagen" block of the digest and `cases/review_items.jsonl`. The class comes from keywords on the first page: "orderbevestiging", "order confirmation", "auftragsbestätigung" and similar make it a confirmation; a title line beginning with "Inkooporder", "Purchase order" or "Bestellung" a PO; "pakbon", "factuur", "offerte" and similar the other classes. With `po_layout` set, a PDF that fits the layout is a PO before any keyword counts, with the two exceptions of section 3.12. Without a keyword the document is "other"; without a text layer it is "no text layer; class unknown" (with `"reader": "llm"` Copilot is asked to class those from the file name and the e-mail's text). The PO number comes from `po_number_pattern` in the e-mail and on the first page. Two or more different numbers: the one the PO store holds with one of its order's item codes on the first page counts; when none of them counts, or more than one, "more than one PO number", skipped and listed. None: a number the store holds that stands on the first page with one of its order's item codes counts, then the one order that holds two of the document's item codes; else the case is opened without a PO, which goes to review as "no version selected" for the buyer to decide (section 3.17 says when the runner still links it by itself). A case linked by a held number or by its lines comes to the buyer with the reason "Kijk na of dit de goede inkooporder is: de assistent vond haar niet aan het nummer achter een van de woorden van de nummerherkenning." (section 2.4): when one supplier's confirmations keep coming with it, add that supplier's word to the number recognition. If the suppliers print the number after other words, or the company's PO numbers have another number of digits (out of the box 6 to 8 after one of five words; a longer number is not taken at all), set the words and the digits at the end of part 1 of "Koppeling met Exact" on the tab "Instellingen" (section 3.17). A file that raised an error stays in the inbox, is tried again at every pass and is logged once in `runner.log` as `scan <file>`.

**A case waits for its purchase order.** The tab "Beoordelen" says above its documents which order, or which version of it, the aid does not know yet ("Inkooporder 250101 kent de assistent nog niet. ..."), and "Zaken die op hun inkooporder wachten" under "Stand van de koppeling" counts it. Nothing needs deciding: when that order or version is filed, the runner checks the confirmation again by itself (section 3.17). At the first pass after the runner starts every order of the store counts as filed, so a restart checks it again only when its order, or the version it quotes, is in the store already. If the order stays away, check that Exact's e-mail with the PDF reached the folder the aid reads, and drag it there, out of the folder and in again when it lay there before the mailbox was connected (section 3.3); and look at "Stand van de koppeling": a PO PDF that does not fit the layout stays in `inbox/` as a file the aid could not read (section 3.12). A case that quoted a version of its order waits for that version. When the number in the hint is not one of the company's order numbers, the aid took another number of the confirmation for the order number: decide on the case on the review page, and set the number recognition (section 3.17). A changed recognition counts for documents that come in after the change; it links no case that came in before it. A case on which a decision was recorded is not checked again: decide on it on the review page.

**A write was refused.** The Exact writer raised a WriterError (no token, a status that is not 2xx, a value that reads back different, no connection). After two attempts the change is prepared instead: it is in `prepared_changes.csv`, the write-log entry has the `error`, and the case is in review with "prepared change: apply by hand", while every later change of that case is "not applied: an earlier write failed". Check the token, the route and the endpoints; the buyer enters the prepared change from the tab "In Exact invoeren" (section 2.6) and closes the case.

**A question was not sent.** The case is in review with "no address for the supplier" (add its address on the settings tab under "Leveranciers", section 2.7, or in `supplier_emails`) or "message not sent" (the mail server refused it, or, through the work mailbox, the Microsoft sign-in has lapsed: sign in again on the tab "Instellingen", in the part "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst" at its bottom, and send the message by hand; see the case's `audit.json`, `messages`). If questions appear as files in `cases/outbox/` instead of being sent, SMTP is not set, or `AID_MAIL_PASSWORD` was missing when the runner started. Questions asked from the review page are always written to `cases/outbox/` today (section 3.7).

**A reply was not filed.** A reply is filed only when its `In-Reply-To` or `References` header names the Message-ID of a question the aid sent (recorded in the case's `messages/sent.jsonl`). A reply written as a new e-mail, or a forward that drops these headers, is not filed: it is handled like any other e-mail. The mailbox rule must save the reply as a whole `.eml` file. A reply to a re-approval request is never filed to a case.

**The page shows no cases.** The page lists only cases in review and cases with a change made in the system that is not undone (whatever their status). Check that the page was started with the same settings file as the runner, press "Lijst vernieuwen", and look at the digest: cases that wait for a supplier, closed cases and failed cases are not listed unless they hold such a change.

**The digest did not come.** The daily digest is made at the first pass at or after `digest_time` (host time), once a day. If `last_sent` in `cases/digest_state.json` is today, it was made already. Look for `cases/digests/<day>.html` and, without SMTP, an `.eml` in `cases/outbox/`. A failed digest is logged in `runner.log` as `digest` and made again at the next pass. In `per_case` mode a pass without cases or skipped files makes no digest. The runner must be running at that time.

**"Postvak niet verbonden" in the digest.** The runner could not read the work mailbox because the Microsoft sign-in has lapsed or the account was signed out; `runner.log` says `mailbox: not connected` at most once an hour. The digest's line ends "onderaan bij Koppelingen": the buttons are at the bottom of the tab "Instellingen", in the part "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst", which opens with a click on its heading. That part still names the account, and says "Aanmelding van ... verlopen" when you press "Verbinding testen" or "Mappen ophalen". Meanwhile the runner handles `inbox/` as usual, a digest it cannot send through the mailbox is written into `cases/outbox/`, and a supplier question or re-approval request is not sent: its case comes to the review page with "Bericht niet verzonden", for you to send by hand (no other way is tried, section 3.14). Open the tab "Instellingen" of the review page on the PC where the runner runs, as the runner's Windows user, open "Koppelingen" at its bottom and press "Aanmelden bij Microsoft" (or "Aanmelden met een code"). At its next pass the runner reads the mailbox again from where it stopped and logs `mailbox: signed in again`, its next messages go through the mailbox again, and the line is gone from the next digest. If the line stays however often you sign in, the runner runs as another Windows user than the page (section 3.14). After "Afmelden" without "Opslaan" the line stays on purpose (section 3.14). Other problems of the mailbox are in `runner.log` under `mailbox`, once in a run of the runner and again after the folder or the mailbox changes: `mail folder not found` (the folder was deleted: choose a folder on the tab and save), `HTTP 429` or `HTTP 503` (Microsoft asked the aid to wait: the next pass asks again), `HTTP 403` (the account has no access to that mailbox in Exchange or, for another mailbox than its own, the app registration lacks the shared permissions or the sign-in was made before the mailbox was filled in: section 3.14), `unexpected link host` (the state file named a link outside Microsoft Graph: it is dropped, and the next pass connects again), `MicrosoftUnreachable: ConnectionError` or `Timeout` (no network to Microsoft: the next pass tries again, and a lapse noted before stays noted), `StoreRefused: ...` (the runner may not use the store of the tokens: it runs as another user, or in another kind of session, than the review page that made the store, for example a scheduled task without the desktop's keychain or keyring, or the store's folder lies inside a git repository; start both the same way, or delete the store's folder named in the line and sign in again from a page started like the runner). The runner goes on with `inbox/` in each case.

**The banner is red or does not appear.** "Inkoopassistent niet bereikbaar": the capture server does not run, or the extension's address is not the server's. "Inkoopassistent weigert de pagina": the reason follows; "missing or wrong token" means the token of the options is not `capture_token`, and "no cart table in ..." means the extension saw a cart table that the cart reader cannot read: the page is kept as `cases/captures/nocart_<...>`, the page to extend the cart reader with, and `cases/capture.log` names it. "Voorcontrole mislukt": with "the pre-check failed; see capture.log" the server met an error, whose traceback is in `cases/capture.log`; with "Herlaad de pagina" the extension was updated while the page was open, no case exists, and a reload is all it needs; otherwise the case's run failed, and its folder says why, as under "A case failed". No banner at all: the shop is not in the extension's list, its access was not granted (the options page names it), its tab was open before the shop was added (reload it), or the page holds no table the extension recognises as a cart. Such a page is never sent, so nothing is kept: save it by hand (Ctrl+S) to extend the cart reader with it.

**"Copilot refused: 403" on the settings tab or in `runner.log`.** Copilot does not let the account use the Chat API. Check, in this order: the account has a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence; the app registration has the seven permissions of section 3.15; an administrator has pressed "Grant admin consent" for the four that need it; and the account signed in for Copilot after those permissions were added (press "Aanmelden voor Copilot" again; like "Copilot testen" it is in the part "Koppelingen" at the bottom of the tab "Instellingen"). The code after the status is Graph's own (for example `Forbidden`); the aid never shows Graph's message. Other statuses: `429`, `503` and `504` (Copilot is busy: the case is tried again at the next pass), `400` (Copilot did not take the request; keep `runner.log` and the case folder for the researcher; a 400 when the conversation is created means the API itself changed). "Copilot gave no answer" fails the reading of that case. "Copilot ended the conversation: disengagedForRai" means Copilot refused that document's content itself; see section 3.15, "When Copilot fails". The digest names a failing Copilot in one line, "Copilot werkt niet", with the number of cases waiting for it, and the documents Copilot could not read while it read others in another, "Copilot kon ... niet lezen"; such a document is finished as failed after its third failure. A `SignInNeeded` under a case id means the sign-in has lapsed, or that the runner runs as another Windows user than the review page and cannot read its tokens (section 3.4): press "Aanmelden voor Copilot" as the runner's Windows user; the case is read at the next pass after that. To work without Copilot meanwhile, switch back to the parser (section 3.15).

**The runner stopped.** If it printed "stopped by .../STOP", the STOP file exists: delete it and start again. Exit code 2 with "aid.runner: ..." means the settings file cannot be read or is not valid (the message names the fields), or `reader` is `"llm"` without the tenant and the client ("reader 'llm' needs the Copilot sign-in": fill them in on the settings tab, in the part "Koppelingen" at its bottom, save and sign in for Copilot). Otherwise see `cases/runner.log` and the log of the scheduled task or service on the host.

## 6. The evaluation commands

**The batch** runs a list of cases headless:

```bash
python3 -m aid.batch --cases <cases.csv> --po-dir <folder> --settings <file> --reader parser|recorded [--recorded-dir <folder>] --out <summary.csv>
```

`cases.csv` has the columns `case_id` (the evaluation's id), `document` (the supplier's document, relative to the folder of `cases.csv` or absolute), `po_number` (blank when unknown) and `kind` (`confirmation` or `cart`). First every `*_po_v*.pdf` and then every `*_export.csv` of `--po-dir` is filed into the PO store, an export as looks at its POs, with the settings `export_columns`, `export_decimal_comma` and `export_fill_down` (see "The export of PO lines" below). `--reader recorded` replays recorded AI answers from `--recorded-dir` (made-up documents only). A cart saved as HTML is read by the cart reader under either reader. The batch prepares every change in `<cases_root>/prepared/`, sends nothing, and ends the waits that are over. `--out` gets one row per case: `case_id`, `status`, `version`, `flags`, `uncompared`, `actions`, `writes`, `messages`, `review_items`, `error`. Exit code 1 when a case failed. To try it on the made-up set: `python3 -m aid.demo --out <folder>` writes the settings, the `cases.csv` of the 22 made-up cases and runs them (options: `--out`, default `demo`; `--reader parser|recorded|anthropic`, default `parser`, where `anthropic` calls the development AI service with the key in the environment variable `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` and is for the made-up documents only, never for company documents; `--force`; `--prepare-only`, which writes the folder and files the purchase orders but runs no case, the folder to watch the runner loop work by itself, as the aid README's section "Try it out on the made-up set" shows). A folder that exists and is not empty is refused with exit code 2 unless `--force` is given.

**The export of PO lines** gives a cart pre-check its PO side: a cart is checked before its order is released and sent, so no PO PDF exists yet. It is an overview of the order lines saved from the ERP, read by `aid/readers/po_export.py`. The runner files an export that it finds in `inbox/` or on an e-mail (section 3.13), and the batch every `*_export.csv` of `--po-dir`. Each PO of the file becomes a look at that PO, kept beside its PDF versions as `po_store/<PO>/e<n>/po.json` (section 3.1): a PO whose rows are those of its latest look, each value compared in normal form (a number as a number, so 12.5 and 12.50 are one price), files nothing, and other rows are its next look. An export file is filed once: a file that is the source of a look of a PO, or that filed nothing for the PO because its rows were those of the latest look, files nothing more for that PO, whatever looks came after it. So the same file given again, and a file that stays in `inbox/` and is handled again at every pass, never comes back on top of a newer export. A look is never a version of the PO: a PO PDF that prints its version number is filed as that version whatever looks the PO has, and a confirmation is checked against the PDF versions of its PO, and against its latest look only while the PO has no PDF version. A look reads as the PO it shows: with the number of the latest PDF version of the PO (1 without one), and with that version's supplier when the export names none. A cart is checked against the PO as it stands: its latest look when that was filed after its latest PDF version, else that PDF version, which also counts when the two were filed at the same time. Try the export settings out on an empty PO store: a reading with wrong settings stands as the PO until the export is filed again with the settings right. What a file may look like:

- **The file.** An Excel workbook (.xlsx; its first sheet is read, hidden rows included) or a CSV file in UTF-8, Windows-1252 or UTF-16 with its byte order mark, as some programs save CSV. An old Excel file (.xls) is not read: save it as .xlsx in Excel. The runner takes either from `inbox/` or an e-mail; the batch picks up only files named `*_export.csv`. A CSV file is cut at semicolons, commas or tabs: the aid tries them in that order and takes the first under which a row holds every column title of the map, so a title line above the column titles may hold another mark or none.
- **The columns.** One row per order line. `export_columns` names the column title of each value the aid reads. It must name the PO number (`po_number`) and the item code (`item_code`), and may name `line_id`, `description`, `quantity`, `unit`, `unit_price`, `price_basis`, `discount_pct`, `net_price`, `delivery_date` and `supplier`. Titles are compared without regard to case and to spaces around them. A column the map does not name is not read, and a value the map leaves out is missing on the PO. With the map empty, each column that is named after one of these twelve fields is read, as in the made-up exports; the file must then have the columns `po_number` and `item_code`.
- **The row of column titles** is the first row that holds every title of the map. Rows above it, such as a title or the date of the overview, are passed over. A title of the map that stands twice in that row is refused. The row of column titles repeated lower in the file, as in a report saved page by page or in two exports pasted into one file, is passed over as well.
- **Totals, group rows and text lines.** A blank row is ignored. A filled row without an item code is passed over: a total, a group row, or an order line that has no item code in the ERP, a text line for example, which is then not on the PO the aid knows. When the quantity is read (`quantity` is in the map, or the map is empty and the file has a column `quantity`), a row with an item code and a blank quantity is passed over too, so a total or a group row that prints its word ("Totaal") in the item code column is no order line; a quantity that is a text but no number keeps its line, with the quantity not read. Without `quantity` in the map such a row is read as an order line, so map the quantity when the export prints a total in the item code column.
- **The PO number** stands on every order line; or, with `export_fill_down` set to `true`, once for each PO, on its first line or on a row of its own above its lines (a group row, or a text line without an item code). A line without a PO number then belongs to the PO whose number stands nearest above it, on an order line or on a row that is passed over, but not on a repeated row of column titles. A PO number holds at least one digit: a word in the PO column, such as the "Totaal" of a total, is no PO number, and a line that would take it refuses the file by the row of the word. A PO number names a folder of the PO store, so on any computer it may hold at most 100 characters, none of `: * ? " < > | / \` and no control character, no space at either end and no dot at the end, and may not be a device name of Windows (CON, PRN, AUX, NUL, COM1 to COM9, LPT1 to LPT9, in any case and also with an extension); this holds for the PO number of a PO PDF as well.
- **Line numbers.** With a `line_id` column each line keeps its number; a line whose number is blank, or is the number of an earlier line of the same PO, is refused. Without one the lines of each PO are numbered 1, 2, 3 in the order of the file.
- **The supplier.** With a `supplier` column the PO gets the first supplier its rows name: its lines and, with `export_fill_down`, the row they take the PO number from (a text line or a group row). The column must hold the supplier exactly as the supplier lists of the settings name it (`review_suppliers`, `messages_off_suppliers`, `supplier_emails` and `supplier_lead_times`: the name on the purchase order), because those settings are keyed by that name, and a creditor number or another spelling would leave them out of the pre-check without a word. If the export has no such column, leave `supplier` out of the map. Without one, a PO known from an export has no supplier.
- **Numbers.** A number cell of a workbook is read as the number it holds. A number written as text, and every number of a CSV file, is read with `export_decimal_comma`: with `true`, "1.000" is a thousand and "1,125" one and an eighth; with `false`, the other way round. A discount is a number of percent (5 for five percent). A cell that Excel shows as a percentage holds 0.05 where it shows 5%, and is read as the 5 it shows.
- **The total of a PO** is the sum over its lines of the quantity times the unit price over the price basis; a discount is not taken off. A PO read from an export whose map names `net_price` and not `unit_price` therefore has no total, and with `authorization_limit` set every change the aid would make on it by itself goes to the buyer instead ("PO total unknown", R10).
- **Dates.** A date cell of a workbook is read as its day. A date written as text is read day first (24-11-2026) or year first (2026-11-24), and a time of day behind it is left out. A time or a duration cell (08:30, or a number of hours) is no value in any column.
- **Error cells.** In a workbook, an error cell (#N/A, #VALUE!, #REF!, #DIV/0!, #NAME?, #NUM!, #NULL!), or a text cell that holds one of these codes, is no value in any column; a date cell whose number lies beyond the dates counts as one, and the warning the reading library gives about it is kept from the console. In a CSV file such a code is a text like any other.
- **The PO number, the line number, the item code and the supplier** should be text cells in a workbook. A number cell holds no zeros in front, so a PO number shown as 0250301 is read as 250301 and an item code shown as 000123 as 123; a whole number is read as its digits. A date, a time, a duration or an error cell there (Excel makes a date of an item code such as 12-10 when it opens a CSV file) is no value: a PO number or a line number so kept refuses the file, an item code so kept is not read and its row is passed over, and a supplier so kept is not read.
- **A cell that is no value**, such as a word where a quantity is expected, keeps its text and is not compared; its line stays.
- **Nothing is filled in.** A line that states no unit or no price basis has none.

The whole file is refused, and nothing of it is filed, when the map names a field the aid does not have, or lacks the PO number or the item code; when the file is neither .xlsx nor .csv, is a workbook that cannot be read (a damaged file, or a cell whose content does not fit its type), or is a CSV file cut at a mark or in an encoding the aid does not read (the error then names the marks and the encodings it reads); when a column of the map is in no row of the file, or stands twice in the row of column titles; when a line has an item code and no PO number (unless `export_fill_down` is set and a PO number stands above it); when a PO number is a date, a time or an error cell, holds no digit, or cannot be the name of a folder; or when the line number of a line in a `line_id` column is blank, is a date, a time or an error cell, or is the number of an earlier line of the same PO. The error stops the batch before any case runs; it names the column or the row, where it is known, and never what a cell holds. In `inbox/` the runner meets these refusals in two ways (section 3.13). A file of another type, a file it cannot read as a table, and a file in no row of which the column titles of the map stand, are no export to it: such a `.xlsx` or `.csv` file is skipped as "spreadsheet without the export's column titles" and moved to `inbox/processed/`. The other refusals come after it took the file as an export, and leave the file in `inbox/` with its reason: a title that stands twice, a row with an item code and no PO number, a PO number or a line number that is refused, and a map that lacks the PO number or the item code or names a field the aid does not have. `aid/madeup/tryout/EXPORT_nl.csv` is a made-up export in a Dutch form to try this on: semicolons, decimal commas, a title row above the column titles, the PO number on the first line of each PO only and a total row. It is read with `"export_columns": {"po_number": "Inkooporder", "supplier": "Leverancier", "item_code": "Artikel", "description": "Omschrijving", "quantity": "Aantal", "unit": "Eenheid", "unit_price": "Prijs", "price_basis": "Per", "delivery_date": "Leverdatum"}`, `"export_decimal_comma": true` and `"export_fill_down": true`.

**The disposition log** is each case's `dispositions.csv` with the columns `case_id`, `version_selected`, `line_id`, `field`, `decision`, `source`, `note` (the header of `evaluation/templates/dispositions.csv`). The aid's own decisions read "Exact update executed (R04)", "prepared (R01)", "write failed (...)" and "ask the supplier (R06)"; the buyer's decisions on the page carry "B01": "accept the PO value (B01)", "accept the supplier's value (B01)", "accept (B01)", "ask the supplier (B01)", "unresolved (B01)". The batch writes the `case_id` of `cases.csv`.

**The necessity check** decides whether the AI reader is needed:

```bash
python3 -m aid.evalscripts.necessity --cases <folder> --spec <folder> --recorded <folder> [--out <file>]
```

It has the parser and the AI reader read every `*_conf.pdf`, scores both against the specs and prints the decision (`keep_llm` or `parser_only`). The default output is `aid/evalscripts/results/necessity_<date>.csv`. As the command stands it replays recorded answers; a run on the made-up set is development evidence, not the check. The command replays recorded answers only: the recorded fixtures of the made-up set, or answers recorded with the development client on made-up documents. Recording Copilot's answers is not built yet (the record command of `aid/readers/supplier_llm.py` uses the development client only), so the check on the development cases, which are company documents, waits for it.

**The freeze record**: `python3 -m aid.freeze --settings <file> [--out <file>]` (section 4.8).

The evaluation protocol, with the disposition log (section 6.4) and the handling of the held-out run, is `docs/process/Evaluation_Protocol_v2_Draft_2026-10-21.md` in the thesis repository.

## 7. Korte handleiding voor de inkoper

**Het dagoverzicht.** Elke dag na de ingestelde tijd (standaard 16:30) krijgt u een e-mail "[Inkoopassistent] Dagoverzicht" (en via Teams, als dat is ingesteld). Zolang er wijzigingen klaarstaan om in Exact in te voeren, staat bovenaan "In Exact in te voeren: 2 wijzigingen. Open het tabblad In Exact invoeren:", met de link naar dat tabblad. Staat er "De lijst In Exact invoeren kon niet worden gelezen", open dan het tabblad zelf om te zien wat er nog in te voeren is. Verder staat erin:
- *Behandelde zaken*: wat de assistent heeft gedaan, per inkooporder. Achter een wijziging in Exact staat "ongedaan maken". "(voorbereid)" betekent: nog niet in Exact. Het staat op het tabblad In Exact invoeren. "(zelf in Exact doen)" staat achter de bevestiging en de status Bevestigd: die vinkt u nergens af, dus de bevestiging voegt u zelf toe in Exact en de order zet u daar zelf op Bevestigd, zoals nu. Onderaan het tabblad In Exact invoeren staan ze ook.
- *Wacht op u*: zaken waar u naar moet kijken, met de link "beoordelen".
- *Vraag gesteld aan de leverancier*: zaken die op een antwoord wachten.
- *Mislukt* en *Overgeslagen*: geef deze door aan de onderzoeker.

**De beoordelingspagina.** Klik op "beoordelen", of open http://127.0.0.1:7860 op de computer waar de beoordelingspagina draait (de onderzoeker meldt het adres als de poort anders is). Kies een zaak bij "Zaak". Links ziet u het document van de leverancier, rechts de inkooporder. Daaronder de lijsten "Afwijkingen", "Overeenkomsten" en "Niet te vergelijken". Bij "Besluiten" kiest u per punt (RI1, RI2, ...) een besluit. Met "Taal" schakelt u naar het Engels.

**Een zaak die op haar inkooporder wacht.** Staat boven de documenten "Inkooporder 250101 kent de assistent nog niet." of "Versie 1 van inkooporder 250103 kent de assistent nog niet.", dan hoeft u niets te besluiten: komt die inkooporder of die versie uit Exact binnen, dan controleert de assistent de bevestiging vanzelf opnieuw. Blijft zij weg, sleep dan de pdf of de e-mail van Exact naar de map die de assistent leest. Is het nummer niet een van uw inkoopordernummers, besluit dan zelf over de zaak. Kiest u een besluit bij zo'n zaak, dan verdwijnt de melding en controleert de assistent de zaak niet meer vanzelf opnieuw: dan besluit u over de hele zaak, zoals bij elke andere zaak.

**Een inkooporder die de assistent zelf heeft afgeleid.** Staat bij een zaak "Kijk na of dit de goede inkooporder is: de assistent vond haar niet aan het nummer achter een van de woorden van de nummerherkenning.", dan heeft de assistent de inkooporder afgeleid uit een nummer of uit de artikelen op de bevestiging, en nog niets klaargezet of gevraagd. Vergelijk de inkooporder rechts met de bevestiging en besluit dan per punt zoals altijd. Is het een andere inkooporder, sluit de zaak dan niet af: kies "Onopgelost" en handel de bevestiging zelf af in Exact. Staat er ook "Geen versie van de inkooporder gekozen", dan kent de assistent de versie niet die de bevestiging noemt: rechts staat dan geen inkooporder en er is niets vergeleken. Zo'n zaak krijgt geen melding boven de documenten en telt niet mee bij "Zaken die op hun inkooporder wachten": bij een afgeleide inkooporder belooft de assistent niets. Wel controleert de assistent de zaak vanzelf opnieuw zodra die versie binnenkomt, zolang u er niet over hebt besloten.

**De vier besluiten.**
- *Bevestigen: waarde van de leverancier overnemen*: de waarde van de leverancier gaat in de orderregel (prijs, leverdatum of aantal). Vink "Ook de artikelprijs bijwerken" aan om ook de artikelprijs aan te passen.
- *Waarde van de inkooporder houden*: er verandert niets.
- *Leverancier vragen*: de assistent stuurt de leverancier een standaardvraag, met u in de kopie.
- *Onopgelost*: de zaak blijft open.

**Let op:** de pagina verandert zelf niets in Exact. Een besluit bij het afsluiten wordt een voorbereide wijziging, die op het tabblad "In Exact invoeren" komt; daar ziet u wat u in Exact invoert. Een vraag aan de leverancier of de fiatteur wordt nu nog als e-mailbestand klaargezet (cases/outbox); de onderzoeker verstuurt die e-mails.

Druk daarna op "Zaak afsluiten". Als alles is bevestigd of gehouden, is de zaak afgesloten: voeg dan de bevestiging zelf toe aan de order in Exact en zet de order daar op Bevestigd (in het dagoverzicht staat daarachter "(zelf in Exact doen)"). Ziet u de melding "Deze wijziging stuurt de inkooporder opnieuw door de fiattering", dan vraagt de assistent de fiatteur de order opnieuw goed te keuren.

**Ongedaan maken.** Klik in het dagoverzicht op "ongedaan maken", of kies op de pagina een schrijfactie onder "Schrijfactie". Druk dan op "Ongedaan maken". Pas na die knop wordt de wijziging teruggedraaid. Dat geldt alleen voor een wijziging die een programma in Exact heeft gemaakt. Zolang alle wijzigingen "(voorbereid)" zijn, staat er geen "ongedaan maken" in het dagoverzicht en is de lijst "Schrijfactie" leeg; een voorbereide wijziging die u niet wilt, laat u vallen op het tabblad "In Exact invoeren", en een wijziging die al in Exact staat en terug moet, draait u daar terug met "Terugdraaien in Exact".

**In Exact invoeren.** De assistent verandert zelf niets in Exact. Op het tabblad "In Exact invoeren" van de beoordelingspagina staat wat u in Exact invoert: per inkooporder de regels met de oude en de nieuwe waarde, bijvoorbeeld "Inkooporder 250108 (S08), regel 1 (KM-0801): prijs 12,50 → 12,70", en daarna de artikelprijzen. Het getal achter de naam van het tabblad is het aantal wijzigingen dat nog in te voeren is. Voer de wijziging in Exact in, vink de regel aan en druk op "Aangevinkte regels zijn ingevoerd". Wilt u een wijziging niet in Exact, vink haar dan aan en druk op "Aangevinkte regels niet invoeren": de assistent vraagt er dan niet meer om. Hebt u alles van een inkooporder ingevoerd, kies die dan bij "Blok" en druk op "Alles van dit blok is ingevoerd": dat noteert de regels zoals het tabblad ze liet zien, en een wijziging die daarna binnenkwam, blijft staan. Staat er "Let op: na deze wijziging moet de inkooporder opnieuw gefiatteerd worden.", dan gaat die order na uw wijziging opnieuw door de fiattering. Dezelfde wijziging uit twee bevestigingen staat er één keer; vraagt een nieuwere bevestiging een andere waarde, dan staat alleen die er. Staat er "Er is niets genoteerd", dan waren de aangevinkte regels al afgehandeld of vervangen; de lijst is dan bijgewerkt. Staat er "In Exact invoeren (?)", dan kon de lijst niet worden gelezen: sluit het programma dat het bestand open heeft en druk op "Lijst vernieuwen".

Ziet de assistent uw wijziging in een nieuwe inkooporder-pdf uit Exact, dan vinkt hij haar zelf af; anders vinkt u haar af. Zo'n regel staat dan onder de lijst bij "Afgehandeld in de laatste 14 dagen", met "(gezien in versie 2 van de inkooporder)" erachter. Laat een nieuwe inkooporder-pdf een andere waarde zien dan de oude of de nieuwe, dan staat achter de regel bijvoorbeeld: Exact toont nu 12,80. Hebt u een regel per vergissing afgevinkt of laten vallen, kies die dan bij "Afgehandelde wijziging" en druk op "Weer op de lijst zetten". Moet een wijziging die al in Exact staat terug, druk dan op "Terugdraaien in Exact": de oude waarde komt op de lijst, met "Terugzetten:" ervoor. Voer die in Exact in en vink de regel af. De wijziging zelf blijft daarbij onder "Afgehandeld" staan zoals ze was, naast haar terugzetregel; drukt u er nog eens op "Terugdraaien in Exact", dan staat er "Deze wijziging is al teruggedraaid." Is er voor dezelfde regel een nieuwere wijziging, laat die dan eerst vallen zolang die op de lijst staat; is die al ingevoerd, draai die dan eerst terug en vink het terugzetten af. Daarna kunt u de eerste wijziging terugdraaien. Vraagt die nieuwere wijziging zelf de oude waarde, voer die dan in Exact in en vink die af; terugdraaien is dan niet nodig. Kan de assistent die nieuwere wijziging niet terugdraaien, zet de oude waarde dan zelf terug in Exact. Onderaan staat "Bevestigd door de leverancier (laatste 7 dagen)": de bevestigingen die u in Exact bij de order voegt, waarna u de order op Bevestigd zet. Staat er "alleen bijvoegen: er staat nog iets open" achter, voeg dan alleen de bevestiging bij.

**De vraag aan de leverancier en de wachttijd.** De leverancier krijgt standaard drie werkdagen om te antwoorden (de instelling kan anders zijn) (zaterdag en zondag tellen niet mee, feestdagen wel). Antwoordt de leverancier, komt de bounce terug, of komt er geen antwoord, dan staat de zaak weer bij "Wacht op u". Het antwoord zelf leest u in uw mailbox, want u stond in de kopie. Pas zo nodig de order met de hand aan, kies dan "Bevestigen" of "Houden" en sluit de zaak af.

**Instellingen wijzigen.** Uw eigen regels staan op het tabblad "Instellingen" van de beoordelingspagina; een instellingenbestand hoeft u nooit te openen. Het tabblad laat zien wat er in het instellingenbestand staat, telkens wanneer u de pagina laadt of het tabblad kiest. Bovenaan staat wat de assistent zelf mag afhandelen. De drempel voor de prijs is een percentage en een bedrag in euro per regel: blijft een prijsverschil binnen beide, dan neemt de assistent de prijs van de leverancier over op de inkooporder en verandert ook de prijs van het artikel; een verschil boven de drempel komt naar u. De marge voor de leverdatum is een aantal dagen bovenop de levertijd van de leverancier, geteld vanaf de leverdatum van de regel op de inkooporder. Een voorbeeld: vraagt de regel om levering op 16 november, is de levertijd 10 dagen en de marge 7 dagen, dan neemt de assistent een latere datum over tot en met 3 december, en komt 4 december naar u. Zonder bekende levertijd komt elke latere datum naar u; een eerdere leverdatum neemt de assistent over. Bij "Eerste zaken van een leverancier" vult u in tot hoeveel afgesloten zaken elke zaak van een leverancier naar u komt (de reden heet dan "Nieuwe leverancier"); 0 zet dit uit. Daaronder staan de wachttijd op een antwoord van een leverancier en uw naam onder de berichten, dan het dagoverzicht (elke dag op een vaste tijd of na elke zaak), uw e-mailadres en de taal, en het e-mailadres van de fiatteur. Een percentage of een bedrag heeft hoogstens twee decimalen; schrijf duizend als 1000, want "1.000" wordt geweigerd.

Druk op "Opslaan": de pagina schrijft alleen wat u hebt gewijzigd, en bij "Opgeslagen" gebruikt de assistent het binnen een minuut. Hebt u niets gewijzigd, dan wordt er niets opgeslagen. Klopt een waarde niet, dan wordt niets opgeslagen en noemt de pagina het veld. Wat vóór het opslaan al gecontroleerd was, houdt zijn uitkomst: een zaak die op u wacht houdt haar reden, en een winkelwagen wordt op dezelfde dag pas opnieuw gecontroleerd als de winkelwagen zelf verandert.

Onder "Leveranciers" kiest u een leverancier, of "Leverancier toevoegen" met de naam zoals op de inkooporder, en vult u het e-mailadres voor vragen en de levertijd in. Met het eerste vinkje komt elke zaak van die leverancier naar u. Met het tweede stuurt de assistent die leverancier uit zichzelf geen vragen: die vragen komen dan naar u, en een vraag die u zelf op het tabblad "Beoordelen" kiest ("Leverancier vragen"), gaat wel naar de leverancier. Druk op "Leverancier opslaan" of "Leverancier verwijderen". Een leverancier die al in de lijst staat, kiest u in de lijst; als nieuwe leverancier wordt die naam niet opgeslagen. Een nieuwe leverancier heeft naast de naam een e-mailadres, een levertijd of een vinkje nodig.

Staat bovenaan het tabblad dat het instellingenbestand niet kan worden gelezen, dan kunt u niets opslaan: geef dat door aan de onderzoeker. De fiatteringsgrens is bij de installatie ingesteld. De koppelingen met het postvak en met Copilot staan onderaan het tabblad, onder "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst". Dat deel opent u met een klik op die regel; u hebt het alleen nodig als het dagoverzicht u vraagt om u opnieuw aan te melden. Daarboven staat "Koppeling met Exact (bij de installatie)": daar is bij de installatie ingesteld hoe de inkooporders uit Exact eruitzien. Dat deel hebt u in uw dagelijkse werk niet nodig.

**Het postvak koppelen.** Onderaan het tabblad "Instellingen" van de beoordelingspagina staat "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst"; klik op die regel om het deel te openen. Daar meldt u zich aan met uw Microsoft-werkaccount: "Aanmelden bij Microsoft" opent een browservenster op de pc waar de pagina draait. Kies daarna de map van uw postvak; dat moet een map bovenaan het postvak zijn, naast "Postvak IN", want mappen daarbinnen staan niet in de lijst. In die map moeten de e-mails met de inkooporders, de orderbevestigingen en de antwoorden op de vragen van de assistent komen: de antwoorden bijvoorbeeld met een Outlook-regel op "Vraag over orderbevestiging PO", de rest met regels op de afzenders van Exact en van de leveranciers, of kies "Postvak IN" zelf. Een e-mail die niet in de map komt, wordt niet gecontroleerd. Druk op "Verbinding testen" en dan op "Opslaan". Vanaf dat moment leest de controle die map zelf, bij elke ronde: wat er al in stond laat zij liggen, en zij verplaatst of verwijdert niets. Lag de controle lang stil, dan leest zij daarna wat sinds haar laatste ronde is ontvangen; een ouder bericht dat u in die tijd naar de map verplaatste, verplaatst u eruit en er weer in. Staat in het dagoverzicht "Postvak niet verbonden: meld u opnieuw aan op het tabblad Instellingen, onderaan bij Koppelingen", open dan dat deel onderaan het tabblad en meld u daar opnieuw aan. Zolang de aanmelding verlopen is, verstuurt de assistent geen vragen: die komen op de beoordelingspagina als "Bericht niet verzonden", zodat u ze zelf verstuurt. "Afmelden" alleen laat de map staan, zodat die regel in elk dagoverzicht blijft staan; druk daarna op "Opslaan" om de koppeling helemaal weg te halen. "Afmelden" meldt u ook af voor Copilot: leest de assistent met Copilot, dan wachten alle zaken tot u weer op "Aanmelden voor Copilot" drukt of bij "Documenten lezen met" "zonder AI (parser)" kiest. Koppelt u later opnieuw, dan begint de koppeling op dat moment en blijft wat er intussen in de map kwam liggen.

**De voorcontrole in de browser.** Voor een winkelwagen: maak eerst in Exact de export van de orderregels (Excel of csv) en geef die aan de assistent: mail het bestand naar uzelf, zodat het in de map van uw postvak komt die de assistent leest, of sla het op in de map inbox van de assistent. Binnen een minuut neemt de assistent de export op; vul daarna de winkelwagen. Op de webshops die in de extensie staan, verschijnt boven de winkelwagen een balk van de inkoopassistent, ongeveer een seconde nadat de pagina opent of de winkelwagen verandert. Groen: de winkelwagen komt overeen met de inkooporder (kleine prijsverschillen heeft de assistent zelf verwerkt; die regels staan eronder). Oranje: er zijn verschillen, met een regel per verschil; pas de winkelwagen of de inkooporder aan voordat u bestelt. Een regel met "wijziging voorbereid" hoeft u in de winkelwagen niet te corrigeren: die wijziging staat op het tabblad "In Exact invoeren", waar u haar in Exact invoert. Een regel met "bijgewerkt" heeft een programma al in Exact verwerkt; die hoeft u ook niet te corrigeren. Grijs: er is geen inkooporder gevonden. Staat er bij een regel "de lijst In Exact invoeren kon niet worden gelezen", dan houdt een ander programma de lijst vast, bijvoorbeeld een geopend rekenblad: de balk is dan oranje, ook waar niets verschilt; sluit dat programma en laad de pagina opnieuw. Rood: de controle is mislukt of de assistent is niet bereikbaar; geef dat door aan de onderzoeker. Staat er "Herlaad de pagina", dan is de extensie bijgewerkt: laad de pagina opnieuw. Met "Bekijk de zaak" opent u de zaak op de beoordelingspagina zolang die op u wacht. Opnieuw laden van dezelfde pagina opent geen nieuwe zaak.

**Copilot.** De assistent leest de orderbevestigingen met Microsoft 365 Copilot via uw eigen werkaccount. Daarvoor meldt u zich aan met "Aanmelden voor Copilot", onderaan het tabblad "Instellingen" in het deel "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst". Een gescand document zonder tekst kan Copilot niet lezen, en die zaak ziet u in het dagoverzicht bij "Mislukt". Staat in het dagoverzicht "Copilot werkt niet", open dan dat deel "Koppelingen", meld u opnieuw aan met "Aanmelden voor Copilot", druk op "Copilot testen" en geef het door aan de onderzoeker als het niet lukt. Staat er "Copilot kon 1 document niet lezen", dan probeert de assistent het bij de volgende ronde opnieuw; lukt het na drie keer niet, dan staat die zaak bij "Mislukt" en controleert u dat document zelf.

**De assistent starten en stoppen.** Staat de assistent in een eigen map, met daarin "Inkoopassistent starten", "Inkoopassistent stoppen", "Inkoopassistent niet meer vanzelf starten" en LEESMIJ.txt (sectie 3.16), dan typt u niets. Dubbelklik op "Inkoopassistent starten": er opent een venster met "De inkoopassistent start. Even geduld: de pagina opent vanzelf in uw browser.", na ongeveer een halve minuut opent de pagina in uw browser, en daarna start de assistent vanzelf wanneer u zich op deze pc aanmeldt. Draait de assistent al, dan wordt alleen de pagina geopend. Wilt u de assistent stilzetten, dubbelklik dan op "Inkoopassistent stoppen"; hij blijft uit, ook na opnieuw aanmelden, tot u weer op "Inkoopassistent starten" dubbelklikt. Zegt het venster daarna "De inkoopassistent stopt nog", wacht dan een minuut voordat u de map verplaatst of verwijdert. Met "Inkoopassistent niet meer vanzelf starten" start hij niet meer vanzelf wanneer u zich aanmeldt. In de map data staan de instellingen en alle documenten die de assistent verwerkt: kopieer of verstuur die map niet. Zegt het venster "De inkoopassistent kon niet starten", geef dat dan door aan de onderzoeker; de reden staat in de map data, in logs, in het bestand start.log. Zegt het venster dat de assistent in deze map niet kan schrijven, zet de map dan op een plek waar u mag schrijven, bijvoorbeeld in uw eigen map Documenten. De code voor de uitbreiding in de browser staat onderaan het tabblad "Instellingen", in het deel "Koppelingen: het Microsoft-postvak en de AI-dienst", bij "Code voor de uitbreiding in de browser": plak die in de opties van de uitbreiding.

**De assistent starten vanuit een kopie van het project.** Draait de assistent niet uit zo'n map maar uit een kopie van het project, dan geldt het volgende. Is het automatisch starten bij het aanmelden ingesteld (`--install-autostart`, sectie 3.4.1), dan hoeft u niets te typen: na het aanmelden op Windows starten de controle, de beoordelingspagina en de voorcontrole vanzelf, op de achtergrond en zonder venster. De opdrachten hieronder typt u in een PowerShell-venster, in de map waarin de map `aid` staat; ga daar eerst heen met `cd`, bijvoorbeeld `cd C:\Users\<uw naam>\<projectmap>`, waarbij `<projectmap>` de map is waarin `aid` staat. Wilt u de assistent stilzetten, voer dan `python -m aid.start --settings <map>\settings.json --stop` uit; hij blijft stil, ook na opnieuw aanmelden, tot u `python -m aid.start --settings <map>\settings.json --resume --background` uitvoert. Geef `--resume` dezelfde opties mee als waarmee het automatisch starten is ingesteld (bijvoorbeeld `--testshop` of `--poll-seconds`); anders draait de assistent tot de volgende aanmelding met andere instellingen. Met `--background` start de assistent weer op de achtergrond en mag u het venster daarna sluiten; zonder `--background` draait hij in dat venster en stopt hij zodra u het sluit. Met `--status` ziet u of alles draait. Doet het woord `python` niets in het venster (op Windows kan het een verwijzing naar de Microsoft Store zijn), typ dan in plaats daarvan het volledige pad van Python, bijvoorbeeld `C:\Users\<uw naam>\AppData\Local\Programs\Python\Python311\python.exe -m aid.start ...`.

**Wie belt u?** Bij een vraag, een fout, een zaak bij "Mislukt" of een pagina die niet opent: bel of mail de onderzoeker.
